# Images

Code: `graphics/`, files panel

- **Overview**: a click on a PNG, JPEG, GIF (its first frame), WebP, BMP or ICO in the files panel shows the picture itself, drawn by each window's own terminal, because agents write screenshots, diagrams and icons into the workspace and opening another app to look at one breaks the flow. Three protocols cover the terminals people use: kitty graphics with Unicode placeholders (Ghostty, kitty), iTerm2's inline images (iTerm2, WezTerm, Warp, VS Code with its image setting) and sixel (foot, Konsole, xterm as a VT340); a terminal with none gets, where the picture would be, a message naming what it lacks and how to get it, rather than an empty panel that looks broken. Each window decides for itself at startup (Detection) and the server follows: two windows on one server, say Ghostty and an xterm, each get what their terminal can show, in their own size. The pipeline: the reading thread decodes the file once into a `graphics::Picture` (Decoding and encoding); per window, `App::draw` fits it to that window's room and cell size and draws the cells of its area; the bytes for that window's protocol are made on a job thread and cached; the server writes them into that window's frame and keeps them right while the screen changes (Rendering). SVG stays text: it is source code an agent edits, and drawing it needs a renderer of its own. Animations show their first frame: playing them would mean a stream of frames to every window for an image someone glances at.

- **Detection** (`graphics/detect.rs`, `host/theme.rs`, `client/probe.rs`): each client decides once, at startup, what its own terminal can show and sends it in `Hello` as a `graphics::Support` (the protocol, or the `Missing` reason when there is none; the cell size; whether to wrap for tmux; `id_hi`); the server only follows it, since two clients of one server can sit in different terminals. `detect::decide` is a pure function of `Facts` (the replies, whether the kitty query went out, the window's cell size, what tmux said) and the environment, tested as a table on the replies the bench recorded (Ghostty 1.3.1, kitty 0.45, WezTerm stable and nightly, Konsole 25.12, foot 1.25, xterm 407 with and without `-ti vt340`, each directly, through tmux 3.6 locally and as seen over ssh), and checked live in each of them.
  - **The probe** adds `CSI 16 t` (cell size), XTSMGRAPHICS `CSI ?1;1;0S` and `CSI ?2;1;0S` (sixel colour registers and geometry) and, where it is wanted (below), the kitty query `ESC _G i=31,s=1,v=1,a=q,t=d,f=24;AAAA` ahead of OSC 10/11, OSC 4 for colours 0-15, XTVERSION and DA1. One batch, ending `OSC 4;15;? CSI > q CSI c`, keeps DA1 as the end marker and adds no round trip over ssh. Asking all 256 colours produced several kilobytes of replies against macOS' 1024-byte tty input queue: replies, including DA1, could be lost even while the client was reading; missing DA1 was also observed over ssh to Linux. The batch size test includes 16-bit RGB, shorter RGB and hash colour formats, the graphics reports, a kitty error, a 256-byte terminal version and long DA1, and pins the largest reply at 862 bytes, below 1022. Colours 16-255 keep the emulator's cube and grey defaults (Terminals). The parser reads APC replies (`G i=31;OK`), CSI with private markers, intermediates and empty parameters (kitty's `?62;52;c`, iTerm2's `?1;3;S`), and skips any other reply whole (DA2, DECRQM's `$y`). Strings never nest: an ESC inside one that is not its `ST` drops it and parsing starts again there, so Alt+`_`, Alt+`P` or Alt+`]` typed during the probe cannot swallow the replies after it. It runs right after `ratatui::init()` and before mouse, paste and kitty-keyboard modes are on. Nothing is asked in band once the input thread runs. A probe that ends on its deadline rather than on DA1 still drains until 200 ms pass without a byte (at most 1 s); starts ending on DA1 pay nothing. The input decoder also drops complete OSC 4/10/11 (and turns an OSC 99 answer into a click, see Agent status), XTVERSION, kitty and private CSI replies outside bracketed paste, even if they arrive after the drain or much later. Filtering raw bytes avoids both typing late strings into a pane and crossterm 0.29 buffering a private CSI ending in `S` together with every following click and key until `c` or `u`. A lone Esc or an Alt string prefix at the end of a short read is delivered immediately during ordinary input, as in crossterm. After either probe ends without DA1, the input thread expects late replies for 1 s; dropping a reply renews that window for 1 s. Only in that window, or when a full read may have more bytes waiting, is a trailing Esc held for up to 100 ms; ambiguous string prefixes are held only while more bytes or late replies are expected. Poll waits shrink to the held sequence's remaining time. Two Escs in separate reads remain two key events, including while replies are expected; two Escs in one read retain crossterm's single-event interpretation. Interrupted strings restart at the new ESC. Incomplete replies expire after 1 s idle and non-paste sequences are capped at 4096 bytes. A reply whose prefix was lost, or whose pieces arrive after its expiry or outside the reply window, can still look like ordinary typing: there is no safe way to distinguish its remaining printable text from the user's keys. The smaller batch prevents the known queue overflow, late replies received together are harmless, and preserving ordinary input takes precedence over dropping arbitrary text indefinitely.
  - **The kitty query** goes out only outside a multiplexer the environment shows (`TMUX`, `STY`, `ZELLIJ`, `TERM` `tmux*`/`screen*`): tmux turns an unwrapped APC into the pane's title (`Gi=31,s=1,…` in the status line), screen into its hardstatus. And only when `TERM`, `TERM_PROGRAM`, `KITTY_WINDOW_ID` or `GHOSTTY_RESOURCES_DIR` point at Ghostty, kitty or Rio, the only terminals whose answer changes anything: Konsole and WezTerm answer `OK` and still draw `U=1` at the cursor (bench), so the name decides, never the reply, and the reply only tells a Ghostty with `image-storage-limit = 0` (which then answers nothing) from a working one. Only `OK` counts as an answer; an error is taken as silence (its text stays in the log). Without the query (Ghostty over ssh with `TERM=xterm-256color`), XTVERSION alone decides, so a Ghostty with `image-storage-limit = 0` reached that way shows blank cells and no message: kept, since sending the APC to every terminal without a hint is what the gating avoids. If it went out and XTVERSION then says tmux (`default-terminal` set to `xterm-ghostty`, over ssh), tmux has renamed the pane, so the client sets the title to `cornercase` rather than leave the query's text there (checked live).
  - **Picking**, in this order: `CORNERCASE_IMAGES`, the multiplexer, the terminal's name, DA1. Kitty placeholders only for Ghostty, kitty ≥ 0.28 and Rio ≥ 0.5.27; iTerm2's inline images for iTerm2, WezTerm, Warp, mintty, older Rio, and xterm.js when DA1 has `4`, which its image addon adds (`TERM_PROGRAM` tells Tabby and Hyper from VS Code, so each gets its own setting in the message; Cursor reports `vscode` too and gets VS Code's; over plain ssh the addon's own DA1 `?62;4;9;22c` names xterm.js, which needs no cell size); sixel for Konsole ≥ 22.04, foot, xterm and mlterm when DA1 has `4`, contour, VTE before 0.83.90 with `4`, and any unknown terminal with `4`; a `Missing` otherwise, naming the terminal by brand and version (`xterm 407`, not `XTerm(407)`). The name comes from XTVERSION by prefix, else from the environment, where a hint naming a terminal that answers XTVERSION is ignored when DA1 came without one: `TERM_PROGRAM=ghostty` inherited by an Alacritty started from Ghostty would otherwise get placeholders, drawn as tofu. Sixel needs a cell size (`Missing::NoCellSize`); kitty and iTerm2 images fall back to 8×16. Zellij's DA1 `4` is trusted only from 0.45 (`Zellij(4500)`), which advertises sixel only when the terminal has it; older ones always do. `TERM=screen*` with neither `STY` nor XTVERSION is a multiplexer cornercase cannot name (screen, or a tmux older than 3.2, which has no XTVERSION and nothing this design uses), and gets a message saying so. An unknown `CORNERCASE_IMAGES` value is ignored and written into the log's `probe` line.
  - **tmux** (XTVERSION `tmux …`, or `TMUX` with no reply). Locally, `tmux display-message -p -t $TMUX_PANE '#{pane_tty}|#{allow-passthrough}|#{client_termtype}|#{client_termfeatures}'`, started before the batch and waited for at most 500 ms, names the outer terminal and says whether passthrough is on, without reading stdin. `allow-passthrough` prints `off`/`on`/`all` from 3.4, `0`/`1` in 3.3 (a flag there), and nothing before 3.3, when passthrough was always allowed. A `pane_tty` that is not the client's own tty means `TMUX` was inherited by a terminal started from a tmux pane: it is ignored, else Alacritty (no XTVERSION) would get placeholders decided from the tmux client's Ghostty. Over ssh, where `TMUX` does not arrive, or when that command fails, a wrapped probe (kitty query, XTVERSION, DA1, each in `ESC P tmux;` with ESC doubled) asks the outer terminal, at least 150 ms after entering the alternate screen, since with `on` tmux drops passthrough while the pane's redraw is pending (it got through 16 of 16 times at 0-50 ms on the bench; a dropped probe would show the passthrough message for the client's whole life), and waits twice the batch's round trip plus 300 ms, at most 1 s: silence means passthrough is off, which costs that wait at every start until it is turned on or `CORNERCASE_IMAGES` skips the check. tmux sends the outer terminal's replies to the client's active pane, so a cornercase started in a pane that is not active (`new-window -d`, a layout script) or in a hidden window under `on` hears nothing and its message says so; the late-reply drain protects only cornercase's own pane, and nothing tells that case apart remotely. Kitty placeholders go through (wrapped transmissions, plain cells) for an outer Ghostty, kitty or Rio when passthrough is on, else the passthrough message; otherwise tmux's own sixel, unwrapped, when tmux's DA1 has `4` and the outer terminal has sixel (`client_termfeatures`, or the outer DA1 over ssh), since tmux decodes and redraws it (foot, WezTerm, Konsole and xterm as a VT340 through tmux on the bench); a sixel terminal behind a tmux without sixel (older than 3.4, or built without it) gets `TmuxNoSixel` with tmux's version, since the fix is tmux, not the terminal; iTerm2 images through passthrough are never offered, as tmux's redraws wipe them. tmux inside tmux, GNU screen, and Zellij without sixel get messages.
  - **tmux's clients**: `#{client_termtype}` is the most recently active client of the session, and tmux shows one pane to every client, so placeholders and wrapped images reach all of them, including one that draws tofu; nothing in cornercase can fix that. When the local check was used, every resize (attaching from a terminal of another size sends SIGWINCH to the pane) runs it again on a short-lived thread, one at a time, whose answer comes back through the window's event channel, so input never waits; a changed decision is sent as `ClientMessage::Graphics`. A remote client (the wrapped probe) cannot be asked again, since nothing may be asked in band once the input thread runs. tmux 3.0 and 3.1 have no `client_termtype`, so locally the outer terminal is unknown and the message says so. When tmux does not know the outer terminal's pixel size it reports a 16×32 cell; it is kept, since tmux places its own sixel images with it, and kitty placeholders are scaled by the outer terminal anyway, so the only effect is an image smaller than the panel allows.
  - **Cell size**: `TIOCGWINSZ` pixels over cells (crossterm's `window_size`; OpenSSH forwards them), else the `16 t` reply, the reply winning when both exist and differ (yazi does the same), each only when plausible (2-256 by 4-512 px, at most twice as wide as tall). On a resize the client reads `TIOCGWINSZ` again; a new window cell size means the font changed, so the old `16 t` answer is dropped (it cannot be asked again), and when the decision changes it sends `ClientMessage::Graphics(Support)` before the resize event, so the server lays out with it (Ghostty's font zoom, checked live).
  - **Messages** (`Missing::lines`): lower case, the first line what, the next ones what to do, naming the setting to change (`allow-passthrough`, with `~/.config/tmux/tmux.conf` as the other place tmux reads and `all` marked as tmux 3.4 or later; `enableImages`; `-ti vt340`; `sixel=no`; `image-storage-limit`, which Ghostty applies on a config reload) and the terminal and its version where known. `CORNERCASE_IMAGES=kitty|iterm|sixel|off` in the client's environment overrides the decision and skips the kitty query and the tmux checks, for terminals nothing can recognise (Blink and a-Shell send no XTVERSION) and to turn images off; per client like `COLORTERM`, not a setting, since each client has its own terminal.
  - **Kitty ids and leaving**: `id_hi`, random in 1-255 per client run (`RandomState`, no new crate), is the high byte of every kitty id of that client (`KITTY_LO` the two low bytes), so clients drawing in one outer terminal (tmux panes) do not collide, and the client can delete its own images without the server, whose link may be gone. kitty and Ghostty keep virtual placements and their data across ED 2 and the alternate screen, so on every way out (quit, detach, restart, kill-server, a signal, a remote window giving up, a panic on the main thread) the client writes `a=d,d=I` for both ids, wrapped under tmux, before leaving the alternate screen; a panic on another thread does not end the client, so the hook leaves its images alone. Checked live: after quit, SIGTERM and kill-server, directly and through tmux, Ghostty answers `ENOENT` for the client's ids and still has a control image under another `id_hi`. A killed client leaves its images until the tab closes, and so does one leaving while its tmux window is hidden under `on`, which drops the wrapped deletes like any passthrough (`all` avoids it).
  - **Protocol and log**: `Hello` carries `graphics` and `probe`, one line of what the terminal answered and how long it took, which `server.log` keeps with the decision on the `client attached` line (with `pointer=`, see Layout) (`images=kitty cell=10x19 tmux=none missing=- id_hi=175 probe="da1=62;22;52 kitty=OK 16t=10x19 window=10x19 registers=- geometry=- tmux=- ms=13"`), so a wrong decision in an untested terminal can be fixed from a log; `protocol::VERSION` went to 2 for it (3 since the pointer, see Layout).

- **Decoding and encoding** (`graphics/decode.rs`, `encode.rs`, `kitty.rs`, `iterm.rs`, `sixel.rs`, `tmux.rs`): pure functions with no I/O, run on the reading thread (decoding) and on encode jobs (encoding), never on the server loop.
  - **Sniffing** (`decode::sniff`, which needs the first `decode::HEAD` bytes): the format comes from the content, never the name: the PNG signature, `FF D8 FF`, `GIF87a`/`GIF89a`, `RIFF`…`WEBP`; BMP's `BM` alone would take a text file starting with `BMW` for a bitmap (and show `cannot read this image` instead of the text), so it also needs a known DIB header size at byte 14, and ICO's `00 00 01 00` needs at least one entry with a zero reserved byte and 0 or 1 planes. TIFF, AVIF, SVG and the rest stay text or binary. The name (`PNG`, `JPEG`, `GIF`, `WebP`, `BMP`, `ICO`) is `Picture::format`, which the viewer's header shows.
  - **Decoding** (`decode::decode`): `image` 0.25 with only `png`, `jpeg`, `gif`, `webp`, `bmp` and `ico` (+1.2 MB). The decoder is opened first with only a 256 MiB allocation cap, its size read from the header, and a picture over `LIMIT_SIDE` (16384) per side or whose decoded pixels pass `LIMIT_BYTES` (256 MiB) is refused before any pixel buffer exists, as `too large to preview (W×H)`: a 12 KB PNG can declare 50000×50000, and an OOM kill of the server takes every shell with it. The decoder then gets the full limits for its own buffers. Each format's bomb is a test with a crafted file (PNG `IHDR`, JPEG `SOF0`, GIF screen, WebP `VP8L`, BMP header, a PNG inside an ICO); image-webp wraps a 16384-wide lossless header to width 0, so that one is checked at 16383. EXIF orientation is applied (`into_decoder` → `orientation` → `apply_orientation`, which `decode()` alone skips), on the shrunk pixels so a full-size copy is never made, and `Picture::width`/`height` are the size after it, for the header. Animations give their first frame (`from_decoder` on GIF, animated WebP, APNG), as most terminal image viewers do; playing them would need per-window frame pacing. Pictures larger than `MAX_SIDE` (2048) are shrunk once with an area average (`thumbnail`, the fastest on big sources and the right filter for shrinking: 104 ms for 6000×4000 against 314 ms for Triangle), premultiplied when the source is 8- or 16-bit RGBA so transparent edges do not darken (transparent pixels are usually black); grey with alpha and float images are averaged as they are, so a huge one is never converted to RGBA at full size first. Errors are short and lower case, for `cannot read this image: <reason>`: `too large to preview (W×H)`, `this kind of <format> is not supported`, `the file ends too early`, `the <format> data is damaged`.
  - **Fit** (`encode::fit`): `s = min(1, room·cell / size)`, never enlarging (a 16×16 icon stays 16×16 pixels), `cols = ceil(width / cell width)`, at least one cell, with 8×16 assumed when the terminal gave no cell size. It takes the protocol, unlike the first sketch, because each has a ceiling: kitty's diacritics table numbers 297 rows and columns, iTerm2 (and Warp) index image rows in a byte (255), and a sixel's pixels are the size it is drawn at, so its ceilings must shrink the box rather than the bitmap: the terminal's XTSMGRAPHICS geometry (xterm drops what passes its 1000×1000), `SIXEL_PIXELS` (1.2 MP, about 2 MB for a photo) and whole bands (below). Kitty and iTerm2 scale the bitmap into the cells themselves, so their ceilings never change the cells. `Fit` carries the cell size it used, since the iTerm2 payload is padded to exactly `cols·cell width × rows·cell height`, and `encode::Key` (the payload cache's key) holds the whole `Fit`.
  - **Kitty** (`kitty.rs`): the picture is sent as PNG (`f=100`) rather than the zlib-compressed raw pixels (`f=32`/`f=24`, `o=z`) the research started from: the `png` crate's fast mode filters rows before compressing, so it is both smaller and faster (the research's figures, before base64: an 800×589 photo is 806 KB in 6 ms against 1.34 MB in 34 ms, a screenshot 216 KB against 232 KB), no zlib crate is needed, and every terminal that gets placeholders decodes it (Ghostty, kitty and Rio read `f=100`; the bench showed `a=T,U=1,f=100` in Ghostty 1.3.1 and kitty 0.45). RGB when the picture is opaque, RGBA otherwise; it is never flattened, since the terminal blends it on the cell's background itself. It is sent at the fit's pixel size, or the picture's own pixels when those are fewer (the terminal scales into `c×r`, so enlarging would only cost bytes). `kitty::transmit` cuts the base64 into chunks of `CHUNK` (4096) bytes, all but the last a multiple of 4: the first `a=t,q=2,i=<id>,f=100,m=1`, the middle ones only `m=1,q=2` (the spec allows only `m` and `q` on continuations), the last `m=0,q=2`, one chunk with no `m` at all. Every command carries `q=2`: after startup a reply would reach the input thread and be typed into the pane. `encode` ends with the virtual placement, `a=p,U=1,q=2,i=<id>,p=1,c=<cols>,r=<rows>` (always `p=1`, so placing again replaces it instead of adding one, and the terminal never has to choose); `kitty::place` and `kitty::delete` (`a=d,d=I`, which frees the data too) are there for re-placing and cleaning up, unwrapped (callers wrap them for tmux). `kitty::cell` gives `U+10EEEE` with the row, column and high-byte diacritics in every cell, so a cell drawn alone (ratatui rewrites single cells, overlays cut rows) is still complete, and the id's low byte as a 256-colour foreground, which tmux passes as it is (it reduces 24-bit colours when it does not know the outer terminal has them). The table is kitty's `rowcolumn-diacritics.txt`, checked identical to Ghostty 1.3.1's `graphics_unicode.zig`, sorted (Ghostty searches it by bisection); a test checks that every cell, all 297×297 with three high bytes, is one column wide for ratatui.
  - **iTerm2 inline images** (`iterm.rs`): one `ESC ] 1337 ; File=inline=1;size=<n>;width=<cols>;height=<rows>;doNotMoveCursor=1:<base64> BEL`. The payload is exactly the cell box in the terminal's pixels, the picture centred in it like kitty letterboxes, on the window's background, or transparent when it is unknown: terminals disagree on how `width`/`height` scale and on `preserveAspectRatio` (WezTerm and xterm.js let the width win, Konsole stretches), and a payload with the box's exact shape looks the same in all of them. No `name` (some extensions make iTerm2 render the file as a document), no `preserveAspectRatio`, no `inlineMedia` (Konsole plays it as audio); `size` because xterm.js's addon refuses a file without it; `doNotMoveCursor` is WezTerm's and ignored by the rest. Format: PNG while anything is see-through (padding included); otherwise JPEG at quality 85 for a JPEG source, and for the rest PNG unless it comes out more than twice the JPEG, which catches photos saved as PNG or lossy WebP while flat graphics and screenshots stay sharp. The terminal gets PNG or JPEG whatever the file was: WezTerm draws a WebP as a black box and Konsole draws nothing.
  - **Sixel** (`sixel.rs`): `icy_sixel` (pure Rust, +0.1 MB) with Wu's quantiser and Floyd-Steinberg at 0.875 (as good as libsixel to the eye, 25-35 ms for 800 px), at most 256 colours or the terminal's registers (0, unknown, means 256; at least 2). It writes `ESC P 9;1;0 q "1;1;W;H`: square pixels, raster attributes with the exact size (WezTerm and xterm.js crop to them), and P2=1. The picture is laid on the window's background when it is known, so the payload is opaque (Konsole ignores P2: on the bench it painted the transparent pixels of an uncomposited sixel with colour register 0), else pixels under half alpha stay unpainted. Its height is a whole number of 6-pixel bands (`sixel::bands`, in `fit` and in every smaller retry: at most 5 rows less, the width scaled to keep the shape), because Konsole also paints the unused rows of a partial last band, a 4-pixel strip of register 0 under the picture on the bench (img2sixel's and chafa's sixels leave a strip there too). The final `-` before `ESC \` is cut: it moves the sixel cursor a band down, and terminals that leave the text cursor at the sixel cursor would then scroll on the last rows. No DECSDM (`?80`, whose meaning flipped in xterm 369), `?8452` or `?1070`: the renderer positions with CUP inside DECSC/DECRC.
  - **Size budget** (`encode::BUDGET`, 1 MiB): tmux drops a whole DCS longer than its `input-buffer-size` (1 MiB by default), including the raw sixel its native sixel support decodes, which `encode` cannot tell from a terminal of its own, and every payload has to cross the server's 1 s write timeout to remote windows. A sixel over it is encoded again with 64 colours (`sixel::FEWEST`), then, if still over, at `sqrt(budget / size) · 0.9` the scale, at most three times; an iTerm2 image wrapped for tmux only shrinks. Colours go first because a smaller sixel no longer fills its cells (it sits in their top-left corner) while 64 dithered colours look close to 256: a 1271×936 photo (Retina-sized cells) is 1.7 MB with 256 colours and 1.0 MB with 64, which then fits at full size, where shrinking alone left it at 898×661. Kitty needs no budget: each chunk is wrapped on its own and stays far below.
  - **tmux** (`tmux::wrap`): `ESC P tmux;` + the sequence with every ESC doubled + `ESC \`, one wrap per sequence (each kitty chunk, the placement, a delete), never around the placeholder cells, which tmux carries as text.
  - **Payload sizes** (release build, bytes on the wire with base64 and escapes; `~/.cctmp/images/live/encode/measure.txt` has every image): in an 80×40 room at 10×19-pixel cells (an 800-pixel box), a 1738×1280 photo is 1.0 MB as kitty PNG, 161 KB as iTerm2 JPEG and 804 KB as sixel; a 1998×1250 screenshot 278 KB, 94 KB and 265 KB; a 512×512 16-bit icon 159 KB, 64 KB and 140 KB. At Retina-sized 16×34 cells (a 1280-pixel box) the photo is 2.3 MB, 342 KB and 1.0 MB (64 colours), the screenshot 652 KB, 195 KB and 585 KB. Encoding takes at most ~75 ms at 10×19 and up to ~300 ms at 16×34 (a photo's sixel, encoded twice); decoding a 6000×4000 JPEG (450-550 ms) is the slowest step, and it runs on the reading thread. For the same 24×20-cell box at 10×20-pixel cells, chafa 1.18 sends 519 KB for kitty (raw RGBA) and 512 KB for iTerm2 (an uncompressed TIFF) against 159 KB and 30 KB here; sixel costs about the same per pixel (1.5 bytes against 1.7). Kitty's PNG is the heaviest for photos (kitty takes no JPEG), so it is the one to watch over slow links.
  - **Checked live** on the bench (`~/.cctmp/images/live/encode/`, a small crate that draws `encode`'s output the way the renderer does): every format (PNG, JPEG with EXIF orientation 6, GIF's first frame, lossless and lossy WebP, a 16-bit RGBA icon, an 8×8 icon left at 8×8, alpha) in Ghostty 1.3.1 and kitty 0.45 (placeholders), WezTerm 20240203 (iTerm2 and sixel), Konsole 25.12 (iTerm2 and sixel), foot 1.25 and xterm 407 `-ti vt340` (sixel); tmux 3.6 with passthrough on (placeholders in place in Ghostty and kitty), off (blank cells, no tofu), and tmux's own sixel in foot, WezTerm and xterm; side by side with chafa 1.18 for the same boxes, which looks the same.
  - **Dev builds**: `image`, `png`, `fdeflate`, `zune-jpeg`, `image-webp`, `gif`, `icy_sixel`, `quantette` and `palette` build with `opt-level = 3` in dev, like syntect: unoptimised, quantising a 1 MP photo took seconds, which made the tests slow (one sixel test 6.3 s, 1.5 s now) and the dev binary unusable on real photos.

- **Rendering** (`files/disk.rs`, `ui/files.rs`, `app/images.rs`, `server/images.rs`, `server.rs`):
  - **Reading**: `disk::read` sniffs the first bytes (`decode::sniff`) before the binary check (every image holds a NUL) and decodes on the reading thread it already had, so the server loop never decodes: `Body::Image(Arc<Picture>)`, or `Body::Unreadable { format, bytes, reason }` shown as `cannot read this image: <reason>` (logged at info with the path, never pixels). Images may be up to `MAX_IMAGE_BYTES` (32 MiB), text files still 8 MiB: a phone photo is often 10 MB and decoding is bounded by the decoder's limits anyway; the cap is chosen from the head, read first only for files over 8 MiB. Each read takes a new `Picture::id` from a process-wide counter, so the 1 s change check (size and time, as for text) gives an image an agent rewrites a new id, and every cache and every terminal sees a new picture. The `Arc` keeps one decoded copy (at most 2048 px a side) whatever the windows.
  - **The viewer**: the header reads `PNG · 1280×720 · 245 KB` (the original size, before any downscaling); ` ask agent ` pastes `path ` (agents read images from a path) and ` copy ` copies the path; ` open ` is left out, since an editor cannot show it; an image has no lines, so clicks select nothing and the wheel scrolls nothing. The picture is centred horizontally under the header and top aligned, in the body less a column each side, cut to what that window sees (`ui::files::image_room`: a smaller window is cropped, so its picture is fitted to what it shows), and never on the window's last row: iTerm2 makes an image's rows with line feeds and several terminals add one after a sixel, so an image ending there scrolls the whole screen. It is never enlarged (`encode::fit`): a 16 px icon stays small, as it is.
  - **Per window** (`app::Sight` in, `app::Placed` out): `App::draw` takes what the window can do (its `Support`, its visible rect, its background, and for kitty which of its two ids to use and whether that id already holds this picture) and returns what it placed (`encode::Key`, rect, path, payload if ready, whether its cells are on screen), so the frame differs per window while everything else stays shared. A window without a protocol gets `Missing::lines` in place of the picture (muted, wrapped, a blank line after the first, which says what; the next ones say what to do).
  - **Payloads** are made off the loop: `App::payload` looks the key up in a small cache (`images::Payloads`: 8 payloads and 64 MiB at most, the least recently used going first, the last one always kept) and starts a job under `panics::job` when it is missing, answered by `AppEvent::Encoded`; at most two jobs run, and a key that is not started is asked again by the next draw, so dragging the panel's border runs the latest size rather than a queue of every step. A job that fails or panics marks its key failed (the window says so) instead of retrying every frame. Until its payload comes a window shows blank cells: a placeholder text that flashes for 50 ms was worse than nothing.
  - **Dialogs hide the picture** (`Overlay::is_modal`): the image is drawn as plain blank cells while one is open, since the dialog dims the screen with SGR 2, which no terminal applies to an image, and a bright picture behind a dimmed screen looks like a bug. Menus, the search, the keys menu and toasts are drawn over it like over text.
  - **Kitty** (placeholders): the picture's cells are `U+10EEEE` with its row, column and high-byte diacritics (`kitty::cell`), the low byte of the id as a 256-colour foreground, `ForcedWidth(1)`, so they are plain text to ratatui, tmux and every overlay: a menu covers its cells and the image comes back when they are drawn again, at no cost. Each window has two ids, `id_hi << 24 | 0xF0` and `| 0xF1` (`id_hi` random per window, from Detection), and a new picture takes the one not on screen (`server::images::Graphics::sight`), so the new cells always differ from the old and ratatui rewrites every one of them; the id is chosen before the payload is asked for and kept while the picture shows, since it is part of the payload's key. After the frame, the server writes the payload (transmit and the virtual placement) when this id does not hold the picture yet, or holds it smaller than the new fit; when it holds it as large or larger (the panel got narrower, the window smaller), only a new placement (`kitty::place`, ~60 bytes) and the terminal scales it down; while a larger payload is being made the old one is placed larger meanwhile, so a resize never blanks it. Then it deletes the other id. A picture hidden by a dialog or by the compact menu stays in the terminal; it is deleted once the viewer shows no image (another file, the panel closed, another workspace), and the window deletes both ids when it leaves (Detection), since kitty keeps virtual placements across clears and screen switches. A screen reset (resize, attach, a panic while drawing) costs nothing: the cells are drawn again. Inside tmux a transmit never goes out in the same turn as a reset, but 100 ms later (`Graphics::wake` shortens the server's wait): tmux drops a passthrough sent right after the pane is redrawn.
  - **iTerm2 inline images and sixel** are drawn by the terminal over cells it no longer tracks, so ratatui's diff must leave those cells alone and the server must know when something else drew over them. The picture's cells hold a marker (`U+10FFFD`, which nothing else draws) with `CellDiffOption::Skip`, so the diff never writes them. After every widget, overlay and toast, a pass in the same draw (`app::images::owned`) keeps `Skip` on marker cells and resets the others in the rect to `None`, since `set_symbol` keeps `Skip` and a menu drawn there would otherwise never reach the terminal; the picture is owned when every cell is still a marker. `CropBackend::draw` sees every cell ratatui writes, and one inside the shown rect marks it damaged (a menu or toast drew over it, or Konsole and tmux dropped all of it); a reset of the screen does too. After the frame the server sends the payload when the picture is owned, its payload ready, and its key or rect changed or it was damaged: `CSI ?2026h ESC 7 CSI 0m`, printed spaces over every row (ECH and EL do not erase sixel in xterm, and WezTerm keeps the old text under an iTerm2 image, where it shows through transparency), the cursor to the corner, the payload, `ESC 8 CSI ?2026l`, so the blank-then-image never shows where synchronized output is known. While something covers it nothing is sent; the uncovered part stays where the terminal erases per cell, and the whole picture comes back once it is owned again, so a toast costs one more send when it goes. When the picture leaves, its marker cells become ordinary cells that differ from the last frame's, so ratatui writes them and erases it like text; nothing else is needed (both protocols live on the alternate screen and go with it).
  - **Two windows**: everything above lives per window in the server's `Client` (`Graphics`: the `Support`, the background for compositing, the kitty ids and what they hold, what was sent and whether it is intact); the payload cache is shared, and two windows with the same terminal, size and background share a sixel or iTerm2 payload (kitty's carry the window's own id). A window that attaches later gets everything sent to it on its first frame.
  - **Big frames and `cornercase remote`**: payloads are sized for the display, not the file (a full-panel photo is ~100-800 KB), but the server writes each window with a 1 s timeout and drops one that makes no progress. Locally that never happens; through `cornercase remote` the proxy used to copy the socket to ssh's stdout in one blocking loop, so a slow link stopped it reading the socket and the server dropped the window. The proxy now reads the socket on a thread into a channel and writes stdout from another (`remote::relay`), so only the proxy buffers and the server always finishes its write. Payload sizes are logged per window at debug (`images` target), each picture shown at info with its path.
