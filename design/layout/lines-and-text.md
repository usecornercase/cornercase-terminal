# Layout › Lines, wording and the colours of text

Part of [Layout](../layout.md).

- **Lines** (column borders, the stack and footer separators, split dividers, the changes panel's rules, tree lines, the usage track) take `View::line()`: palette 239, or 250 on light themes, a step quieter than the muted grey, so structure recedes behind text. Hover and drag still light borders and dividers cyan.
- **Tree lines**: tabs hang from their workspace's arrow, in the workspaces list and in the tree: `├` before each tab, `└` before `+ Tab`, `│` on a tab's other rows (its second line, a compact band), below a compact workspace's name and on a landing line among them (`draw_guide`, `among_tabs`, `tree_among_tabs`). In the tree they sit in the workspace's arrow column; projects and workspaces keep only their arrows, since lines at every level made the tree busy.
- **Empty panes**: a workspace without tabs centers `no tab open` and `+ Tab opens a shell here`; when it holds closed agents (#430) it centers `4 agents closed`, a ` ▶ reopen agents ` pill and the same `+ Tab` line instead (see Saved session). With no project at all the pane shows the logo (the favicon's corner and cursor in half blocks, the corner in the brand blue 27 and the cursor in the terminal's own foreground), the wordmark, the tagline and `+ New opens a folder`, or the words alone when the pane is too short (`draw_welcome`); it needs an empty project list, not just no active project.
  - **`+ Tab` and `+ New` are buttons** (#441): cyan text in the middle of the window that looked like a button and did nothing read as a broken invitation, right where the eye goes after closing the last tab or project. `+ Tab` does what the column's `+ Tab` does (`add_tab`), after `ensure_workspace` so that a project whose last workspace was closed starts its default workspace, as the `new-tab` request does; it is left out while the shown workspace is being removed, like the column's. `+ New` opens `new_project_menu` (open project, new group). Both act on press, like the pill, also in compact mode and without a question (a tab or a menu loses nothing). The words and their columns did not change: the button's rectangle is its label padded one cell on each side (`button_width`), painted `action_style(true)` over the unchanged line when hovered, as ` + New ` looks lit in the sidebar; padding the label itself would have moved the line one column in every snapshot that shows an empty pane. One geometry serves drawing and the click (`ui::no_tab_buttons`, `ui::welcome_button`) through `centered_rows`, which uses ratatui's centring rule (`x + width/2 - w/2`) instead of ratatui's `Paragraph`: the pill's click was computed as `(width - label)/2`, one column left of the drawn pill in an even-width pane with an odd label, and was handed the pane's rectangle where the window's was expected, so reopened agents started with a wrong PTY size. The click is handled in `main_mouse`, which has the window's area. Nothing was added to the socket: `new-tab` already does this, and a menu is TUI-only.
- **Capitals**: the names of the main UI start with a capital letter, so they read as names rather than as words in a sentence: the column titles (`Projects`, `Workspaces`, `Agents`), the sidebar and footer buttons (`+ New`, `+ Tab`, ` Issues `, ` Changes n `, ` Files `, ` TODO `, ` More ▴ `, ` Help `, `‹ Projects`, `Agents ›`), the panels' titles and tabs (` Files `, `TODO`, `+ New TODO`, `Uncommitted` / `Commits` / `All`, like the settings and issues tabs already were) and every dialog title (`New workspace`, `Remove workspace`, `Keys › Git`…). TODO is in capitals, as the word is written in code. Menu entries, the buttons inside dialogs, placeholders, toasts and messages stay lower case: they are actions and sentences, and a menu of capitalised verbs reads louder than its rows. `Overlay::name` and log targets keep their lower-case ids; a rename has its own title (`Target::rename_title`), since `rename_label` is also its menu entry.
- **Expand and collapse**: what the user reads says *expand* and *collapse* for showing and hiding what a row holds: the changes panel's ` expand all ` / ` collapse all ` (it said ` unfold all ` / ` fold all `), the settings note on a collapsed group, the docs on the tree and the changes panel. They are the words review tools use for the same control (GitLab's **Expand all** / **Collapse all** files, GitHub's collapse on a pull request's diffs, JetBrains' trees), and the ones `cornercase status` already printed for a group; *fold* is an editor's word for regions of code (Vim, VS Code's Fold All), and the panel lists files, not regions. The code keeps `fold`, `folded` and `collapsed` as it had them: renaming the model would churn the code and its tests for no change anyone sees.
- **Grey text** (titles, the footer, the search field, arrows, notes) is `View::muted`, never `Color::DarkGray` directly: `ui::muted` keeps the theme's bright black (ANSI 8) when `HostTheme::muted_is_readable`, else palette 243 (245 on light themes). Readable means its WCAG contrast with the background is at least 2:1, or the background is unknown (nothing to compare with, so as before). Some themes make bright black the background or close to it: Solarized Dark (1:1), Warp's adeberry (#2b2b2a on #1d2022, about 1.15:1, the footer and arrows vanished), Nord (1.7:1); One Dark (2.5:1), Dracula (3:1) and Gruvbox (4:1) keep theirs. A known background with an unknown bright black also gets the fixed grey: Warp answers OSC 11 but not OSC 4, and the 256-colour greys are not themed (the surfaces rely on that too). The changes panel's `View` carries the same colour.
- **Soft text** (a row that is not selected, a tab not active, the rest of a breadcrumb, a grey pill, an icon of the icon bar) is `View::soft`, never `Color::Gray` directly: `ui::soft` keeps the theme's white (ANSI 7) when `HostTheme::soft_is_readable`, its contrast being at least 3:1 both with the background (or the background unknown) and with the surface it is also drawn on (palette 236, or 254 on light themes: a selected row, a pill), else palette 250 (240 on light themes, darker there). The surface counts because it is darker than a light background and lighter than a dark one, so a grey that just passes against white (#949494, 3:1) is 2.3:1 on 254. Text that stands out (the selected row, an active tab, a file's name, a header) is the terminal's own foreground (`Color::Reset`), never `Color::White` (ANSI 15), which light themes make white or near it. ANSI 7 is often a light grey on light themes (Solarized Light's is its highlight background, 1.1:1), so rows and pills faded there like the icon bar did (#275). Text that stands out stays `Color::Reset` on a surface too, rather than black or white by `View::light`: some light themes make ANSI 0 a light grey (Tokyo Night Day's `#b4b5b9` is 1.6:1 on 254, where its foreground is 4.6:1). The exceptions are the combo and the queue form, which already picked black or white by `View::light`, and greys used as a background (a hovered grey pill).
- A hovered project, workspace, tab or group row is filled edge to edge with palette 235 (255 on light themes), one step between the background and the surface (`View::row_background`). The active row keeps its surface. No hover fill while an overlay is open or a border or divider is being dragged. The site's palette tints 235 like 236 (`site/src/lib/term/palette.ts`), so it stays between them, and 239, the line colour, so it stays between the surface and the muted grey.
