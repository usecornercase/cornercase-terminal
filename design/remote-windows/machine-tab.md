# Remote windows › A machine's tab

Part of [Remote windows](../remote-windows.md). Picking a machine's workspace, and how a machine's link works, are in Remote windows › Machines' links; the card and the dimmed frame's look are in Layout › A machine's rows and its card.

- **What it is** (#545, `machines/stream.rs`, `machines/stream/`, `control/surface/mirror.rs`, `app/machines/stream.rs`, `ui/remote_tab.rs`; acting on it in `app/machines/typing.rs`, `app/machines/mouse.rs`, `app/machines/drag.rs`): while a window shows a machine's workspace, the pane area shows that workspace's active tab as it is there, streamed by this server, which is the client of the machine's server (the window draws what this server drew, as for a tab here).
- **A view and its surface, not `surface --tab`**: the stream opens a `view` on the tab (`active`, `visible`, at the pane area's size) and asks for `surface` with that view in the envelope. A `surface --tab` never sizes a PTY (see Commands for scripts and agents › Surfaces), and an active view with a size claims its tab (Views › Who sizes a tab), so the panes there take this window's size, and `surface --view` follows the view: another tab of the same workspace is `focus` on the view, and the surface moves with it. Another workspace, or the machine's server restarted, ends the stream and opens a new one. Another client using the machine's own window on that tab follows the views' rule: whoever claims the size last wins, and this window then draws the frame cut or with blank cells, as a tab another view sizes.
- **Three connections per tab** (the view, its surface, the orders), apart from the link's: on one connection answers carry no id, and a stream's lines cannot be told from another answer (why asking a machine has a connection of its own too). Each is `Remote::connect(Use::Link)`, one way like the link, and a `ControlMaster` makes them cheap. The view's connection stays open, since closing it ends the view; its later lines are read only for their `projection`. The orders (`view-set`, `focus`, `redraw`, and what a person does on the tab: `input`, `scroll`, `selection`, `ratio`, `focus --pane`) go one at a time, in the order they were made, on a connection kept between them, made again after a failure. Only `view-set` and `focus` carry the view in the envelope, since the others refuse it; all carry the machine's `boot`. While one is in flight, a new order replaces the one waiting when only the last counts: a size, a tab, a selection, a divider's ratio (for the same divider). Inputs waiting for the same pane at the same size join into one request, so a burst of keys is one round trip, but only while the two weigh at most 1 MiB (each text or paste's bytes plus 64 per event): a request is one `protocol` message of at most 64 MiB, and JSON escaping can grow text six times. Past that the input waits in a request of its own behind the rest, in order. An answer that says the place moved (`conflict`, `not-found`) is dropped. Mixing the streams on one connection by guessing from their shape was rejected.
- **Patches** (`patches: true`): through ssh an agent at 120×40 went from about 348 KiB/s of complete frames to about 15 KiB/s (measured for #334). `Mirror`, which applies `base`, `copy` and `lines`, runs on the stream's thread; a patch whose `base` it does not hold asks for one `redraw`, and a frame it cannot apply ends the stream with the reason. A refused `redraw` opens the view and its surface again, but after `remote::backoff` like any fall, counted as a failure when no frame showed since, so a server that keeps sending patches it cannot base, or refusing the redraw, never makes a loop of ssh connections; a refusal that comes back once that view is gone is ignored.
- **Cleaned on the stream's thread** (`machines/stream/screen.rs`), never on the loop, which only gets whole frames: cells lose control and hidden characters, and panes, rows, cells, rects, dividers and style indexes outside the area or the frame's tables are dropped, a pane named twice is kept once, and at most 256 panes and as many dividers are kept, and panes only while their rects add up to no more cells than the area, since each pane takes its rect's cells in memory even when the frame sends no rows: a frame of up to 64 MiB could list thousands of panes, and 256 of a 1000×500 area alone would take gigabytes. The panes of a split do not overlap, so a real frame's rects never add up to more than its area, so a malformed or hostile frame never panics, never takes the server's memory and never draws outside the pane area (`ui/remote_tab.rs` cuts each pane and divider to it again). A frame from another `boot`, or from a projection older than one an answer already reported (Views › Projection), is dropped, so a tab switched a moment ago never flashes back. That fence belongs to the view it was read for: a projection a late line or answer reports for the view of an earlier try raises nothing, or it would hold back every frame of the new view until the alarm.
- **Drawn like a tab here**: the same cells, rectangles and dividers, the cursor, the `↓ n more` badge and the inactive panes dimmed under `dim_inactive`. The cells come from frames, not from an `Emulator` (see Terminals).
- **Acting on it** (`app/machines/typing.rs`, `app/machines/mouse.rs`, `app/machines/drag.rs`): what reaches a pane here reaches the machine's instead, as orders aimed at the panes of the last frame. The mouse and `selection` carry the `size` that frame gives, so a pane resized there in between answers `conflict` and nothing lands in the wrong cell; keys and pastes carry none, since a resize here (a window or a side panel) changes the PTY there before the next frame, and a burst typed in between would be refused and lost. Keys and pastes go to its active pane as `input` (see Keys and mouse › A machine's tab); the mouse follows the pane's `modes` in the frame, as for a pane here, and the wheel too (see Scrollback). What never reached a pane here (the prefix key, overlays, fields, the side panels' `Esc`) still does not.
  - **A click on another pane** focuses it there (`focus --view --pane`), as here. A right-click on another pane whose program reads the mouse sends `focus` then the press, in that order on the orders connection, as a pane here gets focused and then the press, and the pane is held until the release, so the release reaches it even while the frame still shows the old active pane. A right-click on another pane that does not read the mouse does nothing, since here it opens the pane's menu, which a machine's pane has not got yet.
  - **Selecting**: a left press on the active pane whose program does not read the mouse starts a selection there; each drag sends `selection` with `from` and `to` (the frame then shows it), and the release sends it once more as `Order::Copy`, never with `copy: true`, so the machine's server copies nothing: the text it answers is cleaned (control and hidden characters dropped, line breaks kept) and copied here with OSC 52 and the toast, as a selection here is. Only that last order's text is copied, so a `selection` still in flight at the release is never taken for it. A press released where it was clears the selection there.
  - **Dividers** of the frame are grabbed like ours (`Divider::grab`); each drag sends `ratio` with `split::ratio_at`, in the machine's cells, kept within the least each side takes by the frame's layout (`Node::sides_at`, the leaf minimum when the frame has no layout), and a double-click sends 0.5. `Esc` ends the drag. The one dragged is lit as ours is, and the resize pointer follows only the frame's dividers, never those of the tab here behind it.
  - **Nothing is queued**: before the first frame, and while the link or the stream is down, nothing is sent or kept, and a toast says `vps unreachable: nothing was sent`. Keys kept and sent once it is back could land in another program, after minutes. An order refused with `unavailable` says the same, a `busy` one says the program is not reading what it gets, and the other refusals are only logged.
  - **Left out**, since they act on this computer or are left for later (Changes and Files are #546): a click on a path (it would open Files here) and their underline, the pane's menu and moving panes with the right button, the accounts row under a pane, the `resuming` and `held` bands of `send`, opening OSC 8 links, and pictures.
- **Only while a window is attached**: with none, nothing is on screen, so the three connections close and the view gives the tab's size and its `seen` back there. With one, the view is `visible`, so the tab counts as seen there, as a tab shown here does.
- **Closed or removed there, from its row too** (#544): the stream follows the selection, so it ends when the machine's next snapshot no longer lists the workspace (`follow_selection`, then `follow_stream`), not when the act is answered: the snapshot is what says the workspace is gone, and a removal cut here at its cap may still go on there.
- **Its size follows the pane area**: each change sends one `view-set`, also when a side panel opens or closes; a size asked before the view opened goes out once it has.
- **Down**: when the link falls, the stream ends and the last frame stays, dimmed, under the machine's card with the link's countdown (see Layout › A machine's rows and its card). When only the stream falls, with the link up, it tries again on its own (`remote::backoff`) and the card says `reconnecting…`, also when it fell before its first frame, as the toast for what is typed then says. Once the link is back, the selection follows the workspace (by id on the same `boot`, by path after a restart there) and a new view and stream open, with nothing restarted on either computer. Opening (the view, its surface and the first frame) and each order get 20 s; past that the connection is cut and the reason is `it did not answer in time`. One watchdog thread per stream keeps every deadline, rather than a thread per order, since keys and any-motion moves make orders at the pace a person types or moves.
- **Over the socket**: `status`'s `shown_remote` says the stream's state (`connecting`, `live`, `down`). The local server does not pass the machine's `surface` or `input` on: a client that wants the tab streams it itself with `cornercase --remote DESTINATION view|surface|input` (or the same requests over its own ssh), with the machine's `destination` from `machines` and the ids from `status`'s `machines`. Passing them on through this server would be a large job of its own.
- **Tests**: unit tests drive the stream through an injected `Connect` and an injected `Bell`, which rings an alarm only when the test says so, so no test waits on the wall clock; app tests inject the connector; `tests/e2e/machines.rs` runs it against a second `Session` through `fake_ssh_to`, whose hold file refuses new connections to a destination while the open ones stay up, so a dimmed tab stays on screen until the test lets it reconnect, and what is typed then is shown never to reach the pane there: the orders go in order, so keys kept back would arrive before the line typed once it is live.
- **Not checked live**: a real sshd and `ControlMaster`; only the fake `ssh` of the e2e tests.
