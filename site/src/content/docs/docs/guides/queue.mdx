The queue is the last tab of the [Issues](../issues-and-agents/) dialog: click **`Issues`** at the bottom of the sidebar, then **`Queue`**. It lists the scheduled agents of every project, each with its time, its name, and its state, agent and place. A failed entry is red, a missed one yellow and a started one green. Typing filters the list.

<Screen name="queue" alt="The Queue tab of the Issues dialog with pending, started, failed and missed agents, and the new, start now, remove, clear started and cancel buttons" caption="See what is scheduled and what ran while you were away." />

## Schedule an issue or a prompt

In [Issues](../issues-and-agents/), click **`schedule`** beside **`start`**. Choose the agent and destination as usual, then enter the time. Once saved, the dialog comes back on the **`Queue`** tab with the new entry. GitHub, GitLab, Shortcut, Linear, Jira, Plane and Asana issues use your configured [prompt template](../../reference/prompt/).

For a free prompt, click **`new`** on the **`Queue`** tab and type the prompt. Then choose **When**, **Where**, the **Agent** and its **Mode**: each is a list that opens below it on a click, or with `Space`, `↑` or `↓` when it has the keys. Pick an option with a click or the arrows and `Enter`; typing filters a long list, such as the places. In a repository root, **Open in** chooses a **new tab** or a **new worktree**, and a new worktree asks for its branch beside it. A workspace always gets a new tab. An issue already supplies its usual branch name. Once you have [accounts](../accounts/), an **Account** list beside the agent chooses the account it starts with (the default until you pick another); the entry keeps it, whatever the default is when it runs, and fails and says why if that account was removed meanwhile.

<Screen name="queue-form" alt="The schedule form with the prompt, When set to in 2 hours, Where, Open in a new worktree with its branch, Agent and Mode" caption="Choose a kind of time first, then its values." />

**When** asks the kind of time first, then shows only the values it needs on the row below:

| When | Values | It starts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `at` | a clock, `14:30` | The next 14:30, today or tomorrow. |
| `tomorrow at` | a clock | Tomorrow at that time. |
| `on` | a date, `2026-10-20`, and a clock | That date at that time. |
| `in` | a number and minutes, hours, days or weeks | After that delay. |
| `every` | a number and minutes, hours, days or weeks | A [routine](#routines): repeats on that interval. |
| `every day at`, `every weekday at` | a clock | A routine: every day, or Monday to Friday, at that time. |
| `every week on` | a day and a clock | A routine: that day of every week, at that time. |
| `Claude reset`, `Codex reset` | none | At the next reset of that agent's five-hour plan window. |

The commands take the same times as text: `14:30`, `tomorrow 9:00`, `2026-10-20 09:00`, `in 30m`, `in 2h`, `in 1w`, `claude reset` and `codex reset`. A routine takes `--every` instead (see below).

Clock times use the zone shown in the form, supplied by the client that opened it, including a remote terminal or browser. Dates use that zone's daylight saving rules. A local time that does not exist or occurs twice is rejected; choose another time or a delay. A time such as `02:30` that has already passed today means tomorrow, even on the day the clocks change. The entry is saved as an absolute UTC instant.

A reset choice runs the existing plan usage probe on the server when you save. It freezes the returned reset time; it does not keep polling or follow later changes to the window. If the probe fails or reports no future five-hour reset, the form shows the reason and keeps your text.

Press `Enter` or click **`schedule`** to save; `Esc` or **`cancel`** closes the form (with a list open, `Enter` picks and `Esc` closes only the list). Click a text field to edit it, use the arrow keys (`Ctrl+←` `Ctrl+→` or `Option+←` `Option+→` move by word; `Shift` selects, and the mouse does too, with a right-click menu to copy or cut), `Home`, `End`, `Backspace` and `Delete`, or paste text. `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` move between the fields and lists. A prompt from a template or `--prompt` keeps its line breaks; a pasted text goes in as one line.

**Queued launches always accept the agent's folder trust question**, even with `accept_trust_prompts` off. This lets unattended starts proceed. The form states this before saving. Immediate starts keep your global trust setting. Agent approval modes remain the ones you selected. Queued launches submit their stored prompt automatically, even when automatic submission for immediate issue starts is off.

## When entries run

The server starts every due entry, with no concurrency limit. Entries at the same instant start in insertion order. Each uses the normal worktree, tab and agent launch path and leaves the active pane selected. A toast and desktop notification announce success or failure.

The server must be running, with its session opened at least once after a restart, by a window or by `cornercase start-server`. Closing the UI leaves scheduled work running. The queue does not wake a sleeping machine or start a stopped server.

An entry more than ten minutes late becomes **`missed`** and waits for you. Waking or restarting within ten minutes still starts it. Select an entry to act on it: **`start now`** starts a pending, missed or failed one at once, and **`remove`** removes it; the notice that follows has **`undo`** for six seconds. Click a pending, missed or failed row, or press `Enter` on it, to edit and reschedule it. An entry shows `starting` while its worktree or agent is being prepared; it cannot be cancelled or edited then.

Started entries stay until **`clear started`** or **`remove`** removes them, both undoable from their notice. Click one to close the dialog and jump to its pane while it is still open in that server. After a restart, its historical pane link is unavailable; it cannot point to a different pane with the same id. Removing a started entry does not stop its agent.

The agent command, mode, prompt and destination are captured when scheduled. Editing the prompt or time keeps that agent and mode, even if their settings changed or a custom agent was removed; choosing another agent or mode captures its current command. A closed project or workspace, a workspace that cannot be uniquely identified after restart, or a missing executable fails with a reason. If the server stops partway through starting, the entry asks you to check its workspace before retrying.

## Routines

A **routine** is a queue entry that repeats. Choose **`every`**, **`every day at`**, **`every weekday at`** or **`every week on`** as the kind of time (or `--every` on the command line), and each time the routine fires it starts its agent, or a [flow](../flows/) (choose it in the **Agent** list as `Name (flow)`, or `--flow`; the prompt is the orchestrator's task), in **a new workspace of its own**.

<Screen name="queue-routine" alt="The schedule form with When set to every day at 09:00, Where limited to repository roots, Open in a new worktree in grey, and the checkbox to remove the workspace when it finishes" caption="A routine: each run gets a new worktree, and the checkbox removes it when the run finishes." />

| Repeat | Text | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `every 30m`, `every 2h` | `30m`, `2h` | A fixed time apart, however the clocks change. |
| `every 3d`, `every 2w` | `3d`, `2w` | Every 3 days or 2 weeks, at the clock time you added it; the first run is 3 days or 2 weeks from today. |
| `every day at 09:00` | `'day 09:00'` | Every day at that local time. |
| `every weekday at 09:00` | `'weekday 09:00'` | Monday to Friday. |
| `every week on monday at 09:00` | `'monday 09:00'` | That day of every week. |

- **At least 30 minutes apart, and at most a year**, since each run makes a worktree; the form and the commands refuse anything else and say why.
- Occurrences are counted from the schedule, not from when the last run ended, so they never drift. Days and weeks follow the local clock of the zone shown in the form, so `every day at 09:00` stays at 09:00 across daylight saving changes. Unlike a one-off entry, a local time that does not exist or occurs twice does not fail: it shifts as Jiff's `Compatible` rule does (a 02:30 that does not exist runs at 03:30 that day, and at 02:30 again the next).
- **Each run makes its own workspace.** cornercase fetches the project's remote (`origin`, else its first remote) and makes a new worktree from the remote's default branch, on `routine/<name>-<date>-<time>`, then the project's [setup script](../worktrees/) runs as in any worktree. If the fetch fails (offline, authentication), the run **fails and says why**: it never starts from stale code without telling you. A project without a remote uses its local `main` or `master`. Only git repositories can have routines: a plain folder is refused when you add it. The form's **Where** lists only repository roots, and **Open in** says `new worktree` in grey.
- **A run always starts**, even while the previous one is still working or waiting. Nothing is skipped and nothing is stopped; an interval shorter than the work is yours to fix.
- **The server must be running.** A routine only fires while cornercase's server is up, as for any queued entry: nothing wakes a sleeping machine or starts a stopped server. If the server was down for several occurrences, one **`missed`** run records the gap and the routine goes on with the next occurrence after now; there is no catch-up. A run less than ten minutes late still starts.
- The row of a routine shows its next time (or `paused`), its name and `↻ every day 09:00 · agent · project`. Its **last 10 runs** show under the same name as ordinary rows (`started` with a link to the pane, `failed`, `missed`, `removed` or `kept`); older ones drop out of the list, and dropping a run never stops its agent or removes its workspace. **`pause`** and **`resume`** stop and restart it (resuming picks the next occurrence after now, with no catch-up), **`start now`** runs it once more right away without moving its schedule, a click or `Enter` edits it (the runs to come, not the ones already going), and **`remove`** removes the routine and its runs, with **`undo`**. **`clear started`** clears finished runs, never a routine.
- Each run uses the accounts chosen on the routine (`--account`), checked when it runs: an account removed meanwhile fails that run with the reason. A run starting, failing, missed or kept appears in a toast and a desktop notification, and in Telegram and Discord while you are [away](../telegram/).

### Remove the workspace when it finishes

The checkbox **`remove the workspace when it finishes`** (`--cleanup`, off by default) removes a run's workspace and the branch the routine made once the run is finished:

- **Finished** means the agent's status is `done` and stays `done` for 3 minutes, so a turn that ends while subagents or background work are still running is not cut short. If the agent works again during those 3 minutes, the count starts over. An agent that is `waiting`, has failed or is waiting for review is never removed, and **looking at a run's tab keeps its workspace** (the status then becomes `idle`): the routine leaves it to you. For a flow, finished is the run's end, followed by the same 3 minutes.
- **Only when nothing would be lost.** First comes the same check as the [Worktrees dialog](../worktrees/): nothing uncommitted, nothing unpushed and no lock, in the run's worktree and in every role's. If anything is there, the workspace stays, the run shows `kept · 2 uncommitted, 1 unpushed`, and a toast and notification say `routine <name> left changes in <workspace>`. Otherwise it is removed as in the Worktrees dialog (its teardown run, its shells stopped, the worktree and its folder; a program started outside the panes keeps running, and so does its port), the run shows `removed`, and the local branch is deleted if it holds nothing unpushed.
- The conversation goes with the workspace, since removing it forgets its closed agents: that is what ticking the box asks for.
- An agent cornercase cannot read the status of (a custom command) cannot have the checkbox: it is greyed out with the reason.
- The 3 minutes are counted in memory, by the server that started the run: removing the run from the list, closing its workspace or restarting the server (an update included) cancels the count and the workspace stays. A run that is still counting is not dropped from the last 10.

## Commands

```sh
cornercase queue add --at 'in 2h' --agent claude --prompt 'Fix the login form'
cornercase queue add --at 'tomorrow 9:00' --agent codex --worktree fix-login --prompt 'Fix the login form'
cornercase queue add --at 'claude reset' --agent claude --issue https://github.com/acme/shop/issues/482
cornercase queue add --at 14:30 --agent codex --source linear --issue ENG-123 --workspace 12
cornercase queue add --at 14:30 --agent codex --source asana --issue 1205551234567890 --workspace 12
cornercase queue list
cornercase queue list --json
cornercase queue edit 1 --at 'in 3h' --prompt 'Fix logout too'
cornercase queue edit 1 --name 'Login follow-up'
cornercase queue run 1
cornercase queue remove 1
cornercase queue remove 2 --json
cornercase queue clear-started
cornercase queue clear-started --json
cornercase queue restore 7
cornercase queue add --every 2h --agent claude --prompt 'Check the CI of main'
cornercase queue add --every 'day 09:00' --agent claude --cleanup --prompt 'Review the open pull requests'
cornercase queue add --every 'weekday 09:00' --agent codex --prompt 'Triage the new issues'
cornercase queue add --every 'monday 09:00' --flow Quick --prompt 'Update the dependencies'
cornercase queue pause 3
cornercase queue resume 3
cornercase queue run 3
```

`add` prints the entry's persistent id. `--every` makes a [routine](#routines) and does not mix with `--at`, `--issue`, `--workspace` or `--worktree`; `--project` names its repository, and `--flow` and `--cleanup` (or `--no-cleanup`) belong to routines. `pause` and `resume` take a routine's id, and `run` on a routine starts one more run and prints its id. `queue list` adds ` · ↻ every day 09:00` to a routine and ` · routine 3` to its runs. `--workspace` selects an open workspace; `--project` selects an open project root; `--worktree` selects or creates a branch in that project. Without a destination, a prompt uses the current workspace and an issue uses its usual worktree when the project is a repository root. Use `--source` for ambiguous keys and self-hosted tracker URLs.

An issue URL must belong to the project: GitHub and GitLab issues are read from the project's own repository, and Jira, Plane, Linear, Shortcut and Asana ones from the site or workspace you are connected to, so a URL of another repository or site is refused instead of starting the wrong issue. An Asana link works in its old (`/0/<project>/<task>`) and new (`/1/<workspace>/project/<project>/task/<task>`) forms and must name a task of the account you are connected to. A bare key goes to the only tracker that can read it: `#7` to the project's GitHub or GitLab, `sc-482` to Shortcut, a number to the repository or Shortcut, and keys such as `ENG-7` to Linear, Jira, Plane or Asana. A bare number is never taken for Asana: use `--source asana` to read a task by its id. When two trackers could read it, add `--source`. `--mode` names a configured agent mode, `--account AGENT=ACCOUNT` the [account](../accounts/) that agent starts with (`AGENT=` for its own login; without it, the default when it runs; `edit` changes only the agents it names, and cannot set one back to the default when it runs: remove the entry and add it again), and `--name` sets the entry and tab name. `edit` can omit `--at` to keep the exact scheduled instant; `add` needs `--at` (or `--every`) and either `--prompt` or `--issue`. Editing an entry's `--issue` moves it to the new issue's branch, unless you also pass `--worktree`. `add`, `edit`, `list`, `remove` and `clear-started` support `--json`.

`remove` and `clear-started` print an **undo number**, which is separate from the entry id. `clear-started` removes every started entry, and every finished run of a routine, at once and leaves pending, starting, failed and missed one-off entries and routines alone; removing history does not stop its agents. With `--json`, both answer `{"entries": [...], "undo": 7}` with the complete removed entries. When nothing started, `clear-started` prints nothing, or `{"entries": []}` with `--json`, without using an undo number.

`restore 7` puts that removal back with its original ids, captured prompt, agent, mode, destination, accounts and state, in time and insertion order. Restored pending entries follow the usual scheduling and lateness rules; restoring started history does not start agents again. The server keeps the last 16 nonempty command removals until it stops, independently of the window's six-second undo notice. Undo numbers are opaque and change with the server boot. An undo number works once; an unknown, used or expired number fails. The commands check the server's `queue-undo` [feature](../scripts-and-agents/#ask-the-server-first) before removing or restoring anything.

## Storage

`queue.json` lives next to `session.json`, with its own format version (2 only while it holds a routine, its runs or a `removed` or `kept` state, so an older cornercase finds a version it refuses instead of starting a routine once as a plain entry). For a custom socket whose session is `server.json`, the queue is `server.queue.json`, keeping different servers in the same folder separate. Its entry ids survive restarts and are not reused when an entry is removed. The server saves changes after they settle and flushes the queue on shutdown. A missing, corrupt or unsupported-version file starts an empty queue. An unreadable file is kept unchanged until you change the queue.

The queue is independent of the [TODO list](../todo/). One-off entries run once; a [routine](#routines) repeats.
