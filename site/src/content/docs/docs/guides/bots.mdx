Some work belongs to no project, or to all of them: "review my open pull requests every morning and tell me which ones are blocked", "tell me which issues labelled `bug` came in today, in every project", "start an agent on this issue and tell me when it is done". Until now that meant opening some folder as a fake project, or keeping an agent alive in a pane whose context grows for ever.

A **bot** is an agent of your own for that: a name, a folder, a memory and a chat. It belongs to no project, but it sees all of them and acts on them through the `cornercase` command line. You write to it, one turn of its agent (Claude Code or Codex) runs in a conversation that continues, and the answer lands in its chat.

The window draws a bot as a chat, with bubbles, Markdown and what it is doing, not as the agent's terminal interface: see [In the window](#in-the-window). Everything the window does is also a command, so you can talk to a bot from a script with [`cornercase bot`](#talk-to-a-bot) and a program can follow it through [events](../scripts-and-agents/#bots).

**Making a bot starts nothing and spends no tokens.** It runs only when you write to it, and **only while the server runs**: a turn's process does not survive `cornercase kill-server`, a reboot or an update's restart. A turn that was running then ends with `interrupted by a restart` in its chat, and the messages held for it are dropped.

### The Bots section

Once you have a bot, a **Bots** section sits at the bottom of the sidebar, above the [agents section](../projects-workspaces-tabs/#the-agents-section) when that is on. With no bots it takes no room: the way in is **More ▴ › new bot…**, or `b` in the [keys menu](../../reference/keyboard-and-mouse/#keyboard-shortcuts) (with a prefix key).

- **One row per bot**, in the order you made them: a mark, its name and, dim, its agent and model. The mark is the spinner while the bot answers, and a blue `●` with a count while it has answers you have not read. The row of the bot on screen has the cyan bar `▌`.
- **Click the title** to fold the section into its title row, which then shows the spinner or the unread count of every bot. The fold is saved with your session. **` + `** opens the [new bot form](#make-a-bot-in-the-window).
- **` ⋯ `** at the end of a row (it shows when you hover the row, and always, dimmed, in a [compact](../small-terminals/) layout), or a right click, opens its menu: **rename**, **edit…**, **new conversation**, **open folder** and **remove…**. A bot's row has no `×`: removing a bot deletes its memory, so it asks first. **remove…** says `Remove bot prs? Its chat, its memory and its folder are deleted. Agents it started keep running.` and, while the bot answers, its button reads `stop it first` and stops the turn.
- **The wheel** scrolls the rows when they do not fit.

### The chat

A click on a bot shows its chat **in place of the panes**, and its **card** where the workspaces were: the agent, model, effort, account and access, the folder (a click opens it, or copies its path) and the day it was made. A click on a project, a workspace or a tab goes back to the panes. Showing a bot marks its answers as read, as `cornercase bot seen` does.

From top to bottom:

- **The header** has the bot's name in bold, its agent, model, effort and account, dim, and, at the right, **` new conversation `** (the same as `bot reset`: a `── new conversation ──` line, and the next message starts a new one) and **` ⋯ `**, the bot's menu.
- **The lines**, newest at the bottom. The view stays at the bottom while new lines come; scrolled up, a **` ↓ new `** mark shows when something arrived, and a click on it goes back down. The wheel scrolls.
  - What you wrote is a bubble at the right, at most 80 % of the width, with the time dim after it. A message held for the next turn says `held` instead of the time until it is sent.
  - What the bot answers is at the left, under its name and the time, as Markdown: headings, bold, lists, `code`, highlighted code blocks and links.
  - Each tool the bot uses is a dim line, `▸ cornercase status --json`. A run of more than three folds to its last three, under **`+N steps`**, which a click unfolds.
  - An error is red, with **` retry `**, which sends your last message again, when it is the last line and no turn runs. A notice is centred and dim.
  - While a turn runs, the last line reads `⠋ thinking · 42s · 3 steps` with a **` stop `** button, which ends the turn and everything the bot started in it (`stopping…` until it has).
- **The field** at the bottom is one row that grows to six and then scrolls, with the placeholder `Message prs…` and **` send `** at its right. **`Enter` sends**; a line break comes from a paste, which keeps its breaks. Text sent while the bot answers is held and goes as the next turn. **`Esc` clears** the field; with it empty, `Esc` does nothing. The field takes the same word moves and selection as every [text field](../../reference/keyboard-and-mouse/#text-fields), and **keys go to it, never to a pane**, while a chat shows.
- **A chat with no lines** says what a bot is, in two lines, and offers three examples. A click on one fills the field with it and sends nothing.

A **link** in an answer is **copied**, not opened: a bot's answer is text an agent wrote, and the window never opens a program or a page for it on its own. See [Known limitations](../../help/limitations/).

Each draft stays with its bot: switch to another bot and back and your unsent text is there. A bot's chat is shared by every window attached to the server, as the shown tab is, and reaches a program as `shown_bot` in [`status`](../scripts-and-agents/#bots).

An answer, or an error, that arrives while you are looking at something else makes a toast with a **` go `** button, which shows that bot's chat, and a desktop notification, whose click does the same.

### Make a bot in the window

**` + `** in the section, **More ▴ › new bot…** and `b` in the keys menu open the form:

- **Name**: 1 to 40 characters, not in use by another bot (whatever the case). The server's refusals (a name in use, 50 bots) show under the form.
- **Agent**: Claude Code or Codex. An agent that cannot be a bot is greyed, with why: `not yet`, `not installed`, or, for an access, `its sandbox cannot reach cornercase`. The form is built from what [`cornercase bot agents`](#make-a-bot) answers.
- **Model**: the models the agent suggests, and `default`, the agent's own. Type to filter; `Enter` on a name that matches none uses it as written. **Effort** and **Account** rows show only when the agent takes efforts or you have [accounts](../accounts/) for it.
- **Access**: `through cornercase` or `full access`, with a two-line note under it. The first line says what `through cornercase` is, and the second, in red, what `full access` lets the bot do, see [Access](#access). Codex offers `full access` only, so nothing is chosen for you there and **create** asks you to choose.
- **For**: what the bot is for. It is optional, may be several lines (`Enter` breaks the line here) and becomes `bot.md`.

**create** shows the new bot's chat at once, with its empty state. **edit…** in the bot's menu is the same form with **save**, with the agent grey, since another agent could not continue the conversation. **rename** asks for a new name and keeps the bot's id and folder.

## Make a bot

```sh
cornercase bot agents                     # which agents can be a bot, and why not
cornercase bot new prs --about "Review my open pull requests and tell me which are blocked"
cornercase bot new nightly --agent claude --model sonnet --effort low
```

`bot new` prints the bot's id, a slug of its name that stays when you rename it. The name takes 1 to 40 characters and no two bots share one, whatever the case. There can be at most 50 bots.

- **`--agent`**: `claude` or `codex`. Without it, the agent you chose in Settings when it can be a bot; otherwise the command asks you to say which.
- **`--model`, `--effort`**: as [`start --model`](../scripts-and-agents/#start-an-agent) and `--effort` take them.
- **`--account`**: one of the agent's [accounts](../accounts/); without it, the agent's own login.
- **`--access`**: what the bot may do, see [Access](#access).
- **`--about`**: what the bot is for. It goes into `bot.md` and may be empty.

`cornercase bot edit prs --model opus` changes the name, model, effort, account or access from the bot's next message on; an empty value goes back to the agent's own. The agent cannot change, because another agent cannot continue the conversation: make another bot instead. `cornercase bot remove prs` deletes the bot and its folder, and is refused while it answers.

## Talk to a bot

```sh
cornercase bot say prs "which of my PRs are blocked?" --wait
cornercase bot say prs "and the drafts?"
echo "summarize this log" | cornercase bot say prs - --wait
cornercase bot history prs
cornercase bot list
```

`bot say` writes your message and starts a turn, answering at once with the line's id. With **`--wait`** it waits and prints the bot's answer; it exits with 3 when the turn fails, is stopped or takes more than 15 minutes, with the reason on standard error. A message is at most 20,000 characters; a longer one is refused with its size, not cut.

- **One turn at a time.** A message sent while the bot answers is **held**, and goes with the others held as the next turn. Different bots answer at the same time.
- **The first message of a conversation** opens with what cornercase tells the bot about itself, then its `bot.md` and `memory.md` (each cut to 8 KB), then what you wrote. Later messages continue the same session.
- **Steps stream.** Each tool the bot uses, such as `cornercase status --json` or `Read src/main.rs`, is written to the chat as it happens, so you see what it is doing while it works.
- **`cornercase bot history prs`** prints the chat as you would read it: what you wrote, what the bot used and its answers, with `--last N` and `--after LINE` to cut it.
- **`cornercase bot list`** shows each bot with its state (`idle` or `thinking`), the messages held and the answers you have not read, which `cornercase bot seen prs` marks as read.
- **`cornercase bot reset prs`** forgets the conversation: the next message starts a new one that reads `bot.md` and `memory.md` again. Do it when the context fills up (the error says so) or the bot has lost the thread. `bot reset` and `bot remove` are refused while the bot answers: `cornercase bot stop` comes first.
- **`cornercase bot stop prs`** ends the answer being written, and everything the bot started in it. The conversation stays, so the next message continues it; the messages held are dropped.

An answer, or an error, that nobody waited for makes a toast and a desktop notification (`prs: Two are blocked.`).

## What a bot can see and do

A bot can look at your work with `cornercase status --json` (projects, workspaces, tabs and agents, with their ids), `cornercase search`, `cornercase recap`, `cornercase worktrees list`, `cornercase read --pane ID`, and read-only `git` and `gh`. It acts through cornercase: `cornercase start --project ID --prompt TEXT` starts an agent in a project, and you see it like any other agent; `cornercase send --pane ID` writes to an agent; `cornercase notify` sends a notice. It never changes a project's files itself unless its access is `full` and you asked: it starts an agent to do it.

A bot runs in no pane, so there is no "here". Its commands run with `CORNERCASE_BOT` set, and the command line **refuses** one that would act on "the pane this runs in, else the one shown" unless it names a project, workspace or pane (`a bot runs in no pane: name the place with --workspace ID or --project ID`). It also refuses `kill-server`, `restart` and `update` (`a bot cannot stop its own server`), a bot cannot change a bot's access, and it cannot write to itself.

## Access

A turn runs without its window, so the agent cannot stop halfway to ask you for permission. What it may do is fixed when the bot is made.

| Access | What the bot may do |
| --- | --- |
| **`cornercase`** (the default) | The agent itself reads anything, runs the `cornercase` command line (except `kill-server`, `restart` and `update`), runs read-only `git` (`log`, `show`, `diff`, `status`) and `gh` (`pr list`, `pr view`, `pr checks`, `issue list`, `issue view`), searches the web, and **writes only in its own folder**, `bot.json` and `chat.jsonl` excepted. Your own Claude allow rules apply on top. |
| **`full`** | Change any file and run anything, unattended. Off by default: you ask for it with `cornercase bot new --access full` or `cornercase bot edit ID --access full`, and a bot cannot grant it through `cornercase bot`. |

- **What `cornercase` access is, and is not.** It keeps the agent itself in its folder, but what it drives through `cornercase` is as powerful as what that reaches: it can start agents and type into your panes. The refusals and the denied files prevent mistakes, not a determined bot. Give a bot only the work you would give an assistant at your keyboard.
- **Claude Code** supports both. It loads your own Claude settings, so your MCP servers and `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` work in a bot, and no project's settings do.
- **Codex** can be a bot with **`full` access only**: its sandbox does not let `cornercase` reach the server (checked on Linux; macOS and Windows have not been tried, so they are treated the same). `cornercase bot agents` says so, and `cornercase bot new --agent codex` refuses to start without `--access full`. A Codex bot reads your own Codex configuration.
- The other agents cannot be a bot yet (`not yet`).

<Aside type="caution" title="Full access">
A bot with `full` access can change any file and run any command, with nobody to ask. Give it only to a bot you would trust with an open shell.
</Aside>

## What is in a bot's folder

Each bot has a folder in `bots/` next to `session.json` (`bots/<id>/`, private to you, `0700`):

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `bot.json` | The bot's settings and the id of its conversation. cornercase rewrites it; it does not read it again while it runs. |
| `bot.md` | What the bot is for. You and the bot both write it: it updates it when you give it a job or change one. |
| `memory.md` | What it keeps from one conversation to the next. It adds to it, keeps it short and prunes it. |
| `chat.jsonl` | The chat as cornercase shows it, one JSON line per message, step, answer, error or notice. It survives `reset` and a change of model. |

The chat is cornercase's own, not the agent's transcript, so what other features write to a bot later lands in the same place. cornercase tells each bot about itself in the first message of a conversation and never writes it to the folder, so a new release updates every bot at its next conversation.
