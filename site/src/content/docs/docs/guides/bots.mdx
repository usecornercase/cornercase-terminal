Some work belongs to no project, or to all of them: "review my open pull requests every morning and tell me which ones are blocked", "tell me which issues labelled `bug` came in today, in every project", "start an agent on this issue and tell me when it is done". Until now that meant opening some folder as a fake project, or keeping an agent alive in a pane whose context grows for ever.

A **bot** is an agent of your own for that: a name, a folder, a memory and a chat. It belongs to no project, but it sees all of them and acts on them through the `cornercase` command line. You write to it, one turn of its agent (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, opencode, Cursor CLI or GitHub Copilot CLI) runs in a conversation that continues, and the answer lands in its chat.

The window draws a bot as a chat, with bubbles, Markdown and what it is doing, not as the agent's terminal interface: see [In the window](#in-the-window). Everything the window does is also a command, so you can talk to a bot from a script with [`cornercase bot`](#talk-to-a-bot) and a program can follow it through [events](../scripts-and-agents/#bots).

**A bot made with what it is for sets itself up at once**: its first turn starts as soon as it is made. It reads `bot.md`, schedules what it asks for as [routines](#routines), writes what its runs will need in `memory.md`, and tells you in its chat what it will do, what it scheduled and when that runs next. **A bot made without it starts nothing and spends no tokens.** After that it runs when you write to it and when its routines run, and **only while the server runs**: a turn's process does not survive `cornercase kill-server`, a reboot or an update's restart. A turn that was running then ends with `interrupted by a restart` in its chat, and the messages held for it are dropped.

### The Bots section

Once you have a bot, a **Bots** section sits at the bottom of the sidebar, above the [agents section](../projects-workspaces-tabs/#the-agents-section) when that is on. With no bots it takes no room: the way in is **More ▴ › new bot…**, or `b` in the [keys menu](../../reference/keyboard-and-mouse/#keyboard-shortcuts) (with a prefix key).

- **One row per bot**, in the order you made them: a mark, its name and, dim, its agent and model, or `next 09:00` (`next Mon 09:00` on another day, a date past a week) while the bot is idle, when that fits, and has a [routine](#routines). The mark is the spinner while the bot answers, and a blue `●` with a count while it has answers you have not read. The row of the bot on screen has the cyan bar `▌`.
- **Click the title** to fold the section into its title row, which then shows the spinner or the unread count of every bot. The fold is saved with your session. **` + `** opens the [new bot form](#make-a-bot-in-the-window).
- **` ⋯ `** at the end of a row (it shows when you hover the row, and always, dimmed, in a [compact](../small-terminals/) layout), or a right click, opens its menu: **rename**, **edit…**, **routines…**, **secrets…**, **new conversation**, **open folder** and **remove…**. A bot's row has no `×`: removing a bot deletes its memory, so it asks first. **remove…** says `Remove bot prs? Its chat, its memory and its folder are deleted. Agents it started keep running.` (with its [routines](#routines) named when it has some, and removing them too) and, while the bot answers, its button reads `stop it first` and stops the turn.
- **The wheel** scrolls the rows when they do not fit.

### The chat

A click on a bot shows its chat **in place of the panes**, and its **card** where the workspaces were: the agent, model, effort, account and access, the folder (a click opens it, or copies its path), the day it was made, the agents it started that are still running (**`started`**, each with its status icon, and a click shows that tab) and, two rows each, its [routines](#routines): the name, then `next Mon 09:00 · ↻ weekday 09:00` (or `paused`). A click on a routine opens [its routines](#a-bots-routines), with that one selected. Its **` ⋯ `** (it shows while you hover the routine) or a right click opens its menu: **run now**, **pause** or **resume**, **edit…** and **remove** (with **` undo `** in the notice), the dialog's buttons without the dialog. A click on a project, a workspace or a tab goes back to the panes. Showing a bot marks its answers as read, as `cornercase bot seen` does.

From top to bottom:

- **The header** has the bot's name in bold, its agent, model, effort and account, dim, and, at the right, **` routines `** (the bot's [routines and reminders](#a-bots-routines); it gives way on a narrow chat, and the bot's menu still has **routines…**), **` new conversation `** (the same as `bot reset`: a `── new conversation ──` line, and the next message starts a new one) and **` ⋯ `**, the bot's menu.
- **The lines**, newest at the bottom. The view stays at the bottom while new lines come; scrolled up, a **` ↓ new `** mark shows when something arrived, and a click on it goes back down. The wheel scrolls.
  - What you wrote is a bubble at the right, at most 80 % of the width, with the time dim after it. A message held for the next turn says `held` instead of the time until it is sent.
  - What the bot answers is at the left, under its name and the time, as Markdown: headings, bold, lists, `code`, highlighted code blocks and links.
  - Each tool the bot uses is a dim line, `▸ cornercase status --json`. A run of more than three folds to its last three, under **`+N steps`**, which a click unfolds.
  - A [routine's run](#routines) is one block, wherever its lines came: a bold dim header `↻ Review PRs · 09:00`, its steps folded into one **`+N steps`** row, then its answer. A run that failed shows its error with **` run again `**.
  - An [agent the bot started](#agents-it-starts) is a line of its own: `▶ started claude in web-shop › fix/ci-482`, then `✓ claude finished in …` with the agent's last message under it, or `! claude needs you in …` with its screen. A click on **` show `** shows that agent's tab.
  - An error is red, with **` retry `**, which sends your last message again or, under a setup that failed, runs the setup again, when it is the last line and no turn runs. A notice is centred and dim.
  - While a turn runs, the last line reads `⠋ thinking · 42s · 3 steps` with a **` stop `** button, which ends the turn and everything the bot started in it (`stopping…` until it has).
- **The field** at the bottom is one row that grows to six and then scrolls, with the placeholder `Message prs…` and **` send `** at its right. **`Enter` sends**; a line break comes from a paste, which keeps its breaks. Text sent while the bot answers is held and goes as the next turn. **`Esc` clears** the field; with it empty, `Esc` does nothing. The field takes the same word moves and selection as every [text field](../../reference/keyboard-and-mouse/#text-fields), and **keys go to it, never to a pane**, while a chat shows.
- **A chat with no lines** says what a bot is, in two lines, and offers four examples, the last one asking for a routine. A click on one fills the field with it and sends nothing.

A **link** in an answer asks before it opens: a click on an `http://` or `https://` address shows **Open link**, with who wrote it, the address and where it goes (`goes to example.com`), and ` open ` (or `Enter`) opens it in your browser, ` copy ` copies it. A bot's answer is text an agent wrote, so it never opens on a click alone. It opens only in a window on the server's own computer; in a [remote window](../remote/), the browser or over ssh the click copies the address, and so it does for an address that is not plain ASCII, carries a user name before the host, spells an IP address in a form a browser reads as another one or is over 2,048 characters, with a toast that says why. See [Known limitations](../../help/limitations/).

Each draft stays with its bot: switch to another bot and back and your unsent text is there. A bot's chat is shared by every window attached to the server, as the shown tab is, and reaches a program as `shown_bot` in [`status`](../scripts-and-agents/#bots).

An answer, or an error, that arrives while you are looking at something else makes a toast with a **` go `** button, which shows that bot's chat, and a desktop notification, whose click does the same. Both show the answer's first line as plain text, without its Markdown.

### Make a bot in the window

**` + `** in the section, **More ▴ › new bot…** and `b` in the keys menu open the form:

- **Name**: 1 to 40 characters, not in use by another bot (whatever the case). The server's refusals (a name in use, 50 bots) show under the form.
- **Agent**: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, opencode, Cursor CLI or GitHub Copilot CLI. An agent that cannot be a bot is greyed, with why: `not yet`, `not installed`, or, for an access, why it has no `through cornercase` (`its sandbox cannot reach cornercase`, `a command can write outside its folder`). The form is built from what [`cornercase bot agents`](#make-a-bot) answers.
- **Model**: the models the agent suggests, and `default`, the agent's own. Type to filter; `Enter` on a name that matches none uses it as written. **Effort** and **Account** rows show only when the agent takes efforts or you have [accounts](../accounts/) for it.
- **Access**: `through cornercase` or `full access`, with a two-line note under it. The first line says what `through cornercase` is, and the second, in red, what `full access` lets the bot do, see [Access](#access). Codex, opencode and Cursor CLI offer `full access` only (and Gemini CLI and GitHub Copilot CLI on Windows), so nothing is chosen for you there and **create** asks you to choose.
- **Follow**: a box, checked, that says whether the bot is told when an agent it starts [finishes](#agents-it-starts). `Space` or a click toggles it.
- **Chat apps**: when the bot's answers reach [Telegram and Discord](#on-telegram-and-discord): `while away` (the default), `always` or `off`. With no chat app paired it says `pair Telegram or Discord in Settings › Remote` instead, and saving keeps what the bot had.
- **Secrets** (editing only): the names of the [secrets](#secrets) saved for the bot, such as `2 saved: campus-user, campus-password`, and **manage…**, which opens the Secrets dialog and gets back to the form, with what you changed in it, when you close it.
- **For**: what the bot is for. It is optional, may be several lines (`Enter` breaks the line here) and becomes `bot.md`; filled in, the bot sets itself up for it as soon as it is made.

**create** shows the new bot's chat at once. With **For** filled in, the bot sets itself up there: `── setting up ──`, its steps, then what it will do, what it scheduled and what it needs from you; what you send meanwhile waits for the next turn, which continues the same conversation. With **For** empty the chat shows its empty state. Changing **For** later with **edit…** runs nothing: tell the bot in its chat, or run `cornercase bot setup prs`. **edit…** in the bot's menu is the same form with **save**, with the agent grey, since another agent could not continue the conversation. **rename** asks for a new name and keeps the bot's id and folder.

## Make a bot

```sh
cornercase bot agents                     # which agents can be a bot, and why not
cornercase bot new prs --about "Review my open pull requests and tell me which are blocked"
cornercase bot new nightly --agent claude --model sonnet --effort low
```

`bot new` prints the bot's id, a slug of its name that stays when you rename it. The name takes 1 to 40 characters and no two bots share one, whatever the case. There can be at most 50 bots.

- **`--agent`**: `claude`, `codex`, `gemini`, `opencode`, `cursor` or `copilot`. Without it, the agent you chose in Settings when it can be a bot; otherwise the command asks you to say which.
- **`--model`, `--effort`**: as [`start --model`](../scripts-and-agents/#start-an-agent) and `--effort` take them.
- **`--account`**: one of the agent's [accounts](../accounts/); without it, the agent's own login.
- **`--access`**: what the bot may do, see [Access](#access).
- **`--follow on|off`**: whether the bot is told when an agent it starts finishes (on by default), see [Agents it starts](#agents-it-starts).
- **`--chat-apps away|always|off`**: when its answers reach Telegram and Discord (`away` by default), see [On Telegram and Discord](#on-telegram-and-discord).
- **`--about`**: what the bot is for. It goes into `bot.md` and may be empty. When it is not, the bot sets itself up at once; `bot new` still prints only the id, and `cornercase bot setup prs` runs the setup again, after a failure or after `bot edit --about`.

`cornercase bot edit prs --model opus` changes the name, model, effort, account, access or `--follow` from the bot's next message on, and `--chat-apps` at once; an empty value goes back to the agent's own. `cornercase bot about prs` prints what the bot is for, its `bot.md`. The agent cannot change, because another agent cannot continue the conversation: make another bot instead. `cornercase bot remove prs` deletes the bot, its folder and its [routines](#routines) (it prints how many, and the number `cornercase queue restore` takes to put them back), and is refused while it answers, or while one of its routines does.

## Talk to a bot

```sh
cornercase bot say prs "which of my PRs are blocked?" --wait
cornercase bot say prs "and the drafts?"
echo "summarize this log" | cornercase bot say prs - --wait
cornercase bot history prs
cornercase bot list
cornercase bot setup prs
```

`bot say` writes your message and starts a turn, answering at once with the line's id. With **`--wait`** it waits and prints the bot's answer; it exits with 3 when the turn fails, is stopped or takes more than 15 minutes, with the reason on standard error. A message is at most 20,000 characters; a longer one is refused with its size, not cut.

- **`bot setup`** runs the setup turn again: a new conversation that reads `bot.md` and `memory.md` and sets the bot up. It is refused while the bot answers and when `bot.md` is empty.
- **One turn at a time.** A message sent while the bot answers is **held**, and goes with the others held as the next turn. Different bots answer at the same time.
- **The first message of a conversation** opens with what cornercase tells the bot about itself, then its `bot.md` and `memory.md` (each cut to 8 KB), then what you wrote. Later messages continue the same session.
- **Steps stream.** Each tool the bot uses, such as `cornercase status --json` or `Read src/main.rs`, is written to the chat as it happens, so you see what it is doing while it works.
- **`cornercase bot history prs`** prints the chat as you would read it: what you wrote, what the bot used and its answers, with `--last N` and `--after LINE` to cut it.
- **`cornercase bot list`** shows each bot with its state (`idle` or `thinking`), the messages held and the answers you have not read, which `cornercase bot seen prs` marks as read.
- **`cornercase bot reset prs`** forgets the conversation: the next message starts a new one that reads `bot.md` and `memory.md` again. Do it when the context fills up (the error says so) or the bot has lost the thread. `bot reset` and `bot remove` are refused while the bot answers (`bot remove` also while a routine's run does; `bot reset` does not mind a run, which has its own conversation): `cornercase bot stop` comes first.
- **`cornercase bot stop prs`** ends the answer being written, the runs of its routines that are answering, and everything the bot started in them. With `cornercase` access, whatever the bot left running in the background is also stopped when an answer ends (not always: see the exceptions in [Known limitations](../../help/limitations/)). The conversation stays, so the next message continues it; the messages held are dropped.

An answer, or an error, that nobody waited for makes a toast and a desktop notification (`prs: Two are blocked.`), unless a window shows its chat: cornercase's own, or a program's that [says so with `view-set --bot`](../scripts-and-agents/#a-view-of-your-own).

## Routines

A bot can schedule its own work. Tell it **"review my pull requests every weekday at 9"** and it adds a routine to the [queue](../queue/), with its own destination: the bot instead of a project. Everything about time is the queue's: one-off reminders (`--at 'tomorrow 9:00'`), routines, missed runs, pause and resume, `start now`, the last 10 runs, undo, and the [30-minute minimum](../queue/#routines).

- **A run is a new conversation of the bot**, never the chat's own: it reads the brief, `bot.md` and `memory.md`, then `This is your routine "Review PRs" (every weekday 09:00). …` and your prompt. That is why a bot writes in `memory.md` what its next run needs. A run and a chat turn can run at the same time. It makes no worktree and fetches nothing.
- **It reports in the chat**: the lines of the run arrive as they happen, as one [block](#the-chat). The answer counts as unread and makes a toast, `prs · Review PRs: 3 pull requests wait…`, with a **` go `** that shows the chat at that run (and a desktop notification), unless the chat is on screen. The routine's row in [its routines](#a-bots-routines) says how the last run ended, and a click on it shows the chat at that run.
- **A run that fails says why**: the bot was removed, its agent is not installed, its account is gone, or the turn failed. It makes a toast and a notification, and the chat shows the error with **` run again `**.
- **The next time you write, the bot knows what its routines said**: the answers that its conversation has not seen are put in front of your message, each cut to 4 KB (the newest 10), so "start an agent on the one whose checks fail" works.
- **You can schedule one yourself** with **` + routine `** in [its routines](#a-bots-routines). The schedule form shows the bot in **Where** as fixed grey text (`bot: prs`), and the agent, mode, account and workspace rows give way to `set by the bot`, since a run uses the bot's own agent, model and account.
- **A bot manages only its own entries.** Its commands may add, edit, pause, resume, remove and run the routines of that bot, and are refused for a project's entries and another bot's.
- **Routines run only while the server runs**, as every routine does: a time that passes while it is stopped is a missed run (see [Known limitations](../../help/limitations/)).
- **Removing a bot removes its routines**, with its folder.

**` routines `** in the chat's header, **routines…** in the bot's menu and a click on a routine of its card open the same dialog, **Routines › prs**. It lists the bot's routines and reminders, and nothing else lists them: the Issues modal's **Queue** tab shows the projects' entries only, with a dim line at its end saying how many bot routines there are. Each takes two lines:

- the name, then the repeat and the next run (`↻ weekday 09:00 · next Mon 09:00`, `↻ weekday 09:00 · paused`, `once · Sun 16 Nov 09:00` for a reminder that has not run);
- how its **last run** ended: `answered` in green, `failed` in red, `stopped`, `missed` in yellow, `answering…` while it runs, or `not run yet`, with when.

The selected row (and the one under the pointer) shows its buttons, each only when it applies: **` run now `** (a routine, or a reminder that has not run, failed or was missed), **` pause `** or **` resume `**, **` edit… `** and **` remove `**, which takes the routine with its runs and offers **` undo `** in the notice. `↑` `↓`, `PageUp`, `PageDown`, `Home` and `End` move, `Esc` closes. `Enter`, or a click on a row, shows the chat at the last run's block and closes the dialog; a run that never started or was missed left no block, so the dialog stays and its hint says why. **` + routine `** opens the schedule form for the bot, as an **every day at 09:00** routine (the kind combo gives a reminder), and **` edit… `** opens it filled in. Saving or cancelling comes back to the dialog.

## On Telegram and Discord

With a phone paired on [Telegram](../telegram/) or [Discord](../discord/), a bot's answers reach it too, titled `🤖 PRs` (and `· ↻ Review PRs` for a routine's), with their Markdown; a failure starts with `✗`. What you wrote, its steps and its notices are not sent. When an agent the bot [follows](#agents-it-starts) needs you, its question comes with its buttons, as an agent's does while you are away.

**When** is the bot's `chat_apps` option, in the form's **Chat apps** row or with `--chat-apps`:

- `away` (the default): only while you are [away](../telegram/#go-away), as agents' messages are.
- `always`: at the desk too, for a bot you mostly talk to from the phone.
- `off`: never, for a bot whose routines would be noise there.

**Write to a bot** from the chat: reply to one of its messages, use `/bot PRs what is blocked?`, or `/bots` and its **✎** button. A 👍 says the bot took it, and its answer comes back as a reply to your message, whatever the option says. A reply to a routine's answer goes to the bot's chat, which is told that answer first. With Discord's [forum mode](../discord/#forum-mode) each bot has its own post, `🤖 PRs`, where its answers and its routines' go and where what you write goes to it.

## Agents it starts

When a bot starts an agent (`cornercase start`, and with `full` access also a tab or split that runs one, or a [flow](../flows/)), cornercase remembers which bot did, tells the chat, and follows the agent.

- **The tab says who started it**: `by prs` in the second line of its tab row, the tab bar and its row in the [agents section](../projects-workspaces-tabs/#the-agents-section). The chat gets `▶ started claude in web-shop › fix/ci-482` with **` show `**, which shows that tab; it reads `closed` when the agent is gone, or after a server restart.
- **When the agent's turn ends**, the chat gets `✓ claude finished in …` (or `✗ claude failed in …`) with the agent's last message, cut to 2 KB, under it (six rows, then **`+N lines`**, which a click unfolds), and then the bot gets a turn: it says what matters, checks the pull request or starts the next step, as you asked. The turn continues the conversation that started the agent: the chat's, or, for an agent a [routine's run](#routines) started, the run's. Reports that come within 30 seconds of each other go into one turn, so the lines appear 30 seconds or more after the agent finished.
- **When the agent asks you something**, the chat gets `! claude needs you in …` with its screen, and the bot gets no turn: answer it in the agent's tab, as always. It counts as unread, with no toast. A `cornercase`-access bot cannot answer it: cornercase refuses what it writes to an agent that waits for an answer.
- **Only the first finish after each prompt from the bot is reported.** What you type into that agent yourself changes nothing, so talking to it stays quiet; when the bot writes to it again with `cornercase send --enter`, the next finish is reported.
- **A bot follows at most 20 agents**; a 21st drops the oldest, with a notice in the chat. The tab keeps its `by prs`.
- **`--follow off`**, or the form's **Follow** box, turns it off: the bot is told nothing, the chat gets no lines and no turns start, and the tabs still say `by prs`. The bot's brief says which of the two it is.
- **After a restart** the tabs keep their `by prs`, the old ` show ` links read `closed`, and the bot follows the agents again from its next `send --enter` into them.
- **Not across machines**: a `cornercase`-access bot cannot start one there, and an agent a `full` bot starts with `--machine` or `--remote` is not tagged or followed.

## What a bot can see and do

A bot can look at your work with `cornercase status --json` (projects, workspaces, tabs and agents, with their ids), `cornercase search`, `cornercase recap`, `cornercase worktrees list`, `cornercase read --pane ID`, and read-only `git` in your projects (`git -C <folder> log`, `show`, `diff`, `status`, with the folder from `status --json`; Claude Code only) and `gh` (with `-R`). It acts through cornercase: `cornercase start --project ID --prompt TEXT` starts an agent in a project, and you see it like any other agent; `cornercase send --pane ID --enter` writes a prompt to an agent; `cornercase notify` sends a notice. It never changes a project's files itself unless its access is `full` and you asked: it starts an agent to do it.

Its commands find the programs your `PATH` finds, in the same order (the server's `PATH`, from the shell that started it); only `cornercase` comes first, from a link in [`bin/`](../../reference/files-and-environment/#files), so it is the server's own. Two exceptions: a Codex bot runs its commands through a login shell, which can put your own folders first (it may find an installed `cornercase`), and if the link cannot be made (the log says so) the folder of the server's program goes first instead, which can change the order of other programs.

A bot runs in no pane, so there is no "here". Its commands run with `CORNERCASE_BOT` set, and the command line **refuses** one that would act on "the pane this runs in, else the one shown" unless it names a project, workspace or pane (`a bot runs in no pane: name the place with --workspace ID or --project ID`). It also refuses `kill-server`, `restart` and `update` (`a bot cannot stop its own server`), `config export --to` (`a bot cannot have cornercase write to a file`), opening a window (`cornercase` with no command, `cornercase remote` to a machine and `cornercase proxy`: `a bot cannot attach as a window`), `--remote` and `CORNERCASE_REMOTE` (`a bot works on this computer only: it cannot act on another machine`), `plugin` other than `list`, `status`, `actions` and `logs` (`a bot cannot run or change cornercase's plugins`), `machines add`, `edit`, `remove` and `update`, and `remote --remember` and `--forget` (`a bot cannot change cornercase's machines`) and every `tunnel` command (`a bot cannot open or close a tunnel`); a bot cannot change a bot's access, and it cannot write to itself. Each line of `cornercase requests --follow -` is checked the same way.

With `cornercase` access, **a bot writes prompts to agents, and the server refuses the rest**. The server knows a bot's commands by their process, not by a variable the bot could drop (with the rare exceptions in [Known limitations](../../help/limitations/)), and everything from your own terminal works as before. Each refusal fails with exit 1 and says why:

- **Typing.** `cornercase keys` and `input` are refused outright (`a bot cannot press keys or type as a window does`): a key can answer a permission prompt or change an agent's mode. `send`, `todo send`, `paste-file` and `held --send-now` work only into a pane that runs an agent, and not while that agent asks you something, shows a dialog, a panel or its shell mode, or is starting or resuming, even with `--force` (`a bot can only type into a pane that runs an agent, and …`). A text that starts with `/` or `!`, an agent's own command or its shell, is refused (`a bot writes prompts to an agent, not its commands`), and so are a text holding a control character other than a line break or a tab (`a bot writes prompts to an agent, not keys`) and a `send` with no text. If the agent starts asking, or shows a dialog, between the paste and the Enter, the Enter is not pressed. `new-tab` and `split` with a command are refused, since the command is typed into a new shell.
- **Starting.** `cornercase start` in a mode that never asks before it acts (`skip permissions (dangerous)` and the like, named with `--mode` or set in Settings) is refused: the bot names another, such as `--mode default`. `flow run` is refused, since a flow's agents never ask. Starting an agent, a flow or a workspace with `--machine` is refused: a bot works on this computer only (reading `stats`, `usage` and `machines` of a machine still works). `cornercase agents reopen` is refused when it would bring back a flow's roles or an agent in a mode that never asks.
- **Closing and removing.** A bot closes only the tabs and panes it started; closing a workspace, a project, a group or an agent you started, forgetting closed agents, stopping a process or a port, discarding changes, and removing worktrees or their builds are refused.
- **Changing cornercase.** Its settings, flows (`flow import` and `flow save` only as a preview or a check), plugins, accounts and profiles, machines, tunnels, pairing a device and the Discord server are refused, and so are stopping, restarting or updating cornercase (`a bot cannot stop its own server`) and `cornercase clone` (`a bot cannot clone a repository`).
- **Opening.** `cornercase open` of a bot's folder or a folder inside it, and of a `.code-workspace` file, is refused.
- **A window.** A bot's connection never acts as a window, whose keys and clicks would be yours: it is closed with `a bot cannot attach as a window`.

## Access

A turn runs without its window, so the agent cannot stop halfway to ask you for permission. What it may do is fixed when the bot is made.

| Access | What the bot may do |
| --- | --- |
| **`cornercase`** (the default) | The agent itself reads anything, runs the `cornercase` command line (except `kill-server`, `restart`, `update`, `clone` and `config export --to`), runs read-only `git` in your projects' and worktrees' folders (`git -C <folder> log`, `show`, `diff`, `status`, never `--output`; Claude Code only; with very many projects and worktrees open, and with far fewer on Windows, only the first by folder path, and the server log says how many were left out; a project whose path holds a quote, a backtick, `$`, `!`, a comma, a parenthesis, `*` or `?` gets no git at all, and a backslash does the same outside Windows) and `gh` (`pr list`, `pr view`, `pr checks`, `issue list`, `issue view`), searches the web, and **writes only in its own folder**, `bot.json`, `chat.jsonl` and names starting with `-` at its top excepted. Your own allow rules (Claude's settings, Gemini's policies, Copilot's configuration) apply on top. Gemini CLI and GitHub Copilot CLI read only their own folder. |
| **`full`** | Change any file and run anything, unattended. Off by default: you ask for it with `cornercase bot new --access full` or `cornercase bot edit ID --access full`, and a bot cannot grant it through `cornercase bot`. |

- **What `cornercase` access is, and is not.** It keeps the agent itself in its folder, and the server lets its `cornercase` commands write prompts to agents and refuses the rest listed in [What a bot can see and do](#what-a-bot-can-see-and-do). That is a boundary. These are not: your own Claude allow rules (they apply on top); `plugin`, `--remote` and `config export --to`, which only the command line refuses; a question an agent asks that cornercase does not see (opencode never says it waits), which a bot's prompt can answer; the instant an agent exits, when a bot's pasted prompt can land on its shell's line (its Enter is not pressed); and moving what the window shows (`focus`, `go`), which can put your typing in another pane. Give a bot only the work you would give an assistant at your keyboard.
- **Claude Code** supports both. It loads your own Claude settings, so your MCP servers and `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` work in a bot, and no project's settings do.
- **Codex** can be a bot with **`full` access only**: its sandbox does not let `cornercase` reach the server (checked on Linux; macOS and Windows have not been tried, so they are treated the same). `cornercase bot agents` says so, and `cornercase bot new --agent codex` refuses to start without `--access full`. A Codex bot reads your own Codex configuration.
- **Gemini CLI** supports both on Linux and macOS, and **`full` only on Windows**. With `through cornercase` it reads and writes only its own folder (it cannot read the rest of your disk) and runs no `git`, and its rules are a file, `.gemini/cornercase-policy.toml` in the bot's folder, that cornercase writes again before every turn; the bot cannot change `.gemini/` or write a `.env`. Your own Gemini policies apply on top.
- **GitHub Copilot CLI** supports both on Linux and macOS, and **`full` only on Windows**. With `through cornercase` it reads and writes only its own folder and runs no `git`. It gives no token count for a turn, and an effort needs a model named by you (not `auto`): `cornercase bot new` and the form refuse it otherwise.
- **opencode** and **Cursor CLI** can be a bot with **`full` access only**: their permission rules cannot keep a command's or an edit's writes in the bot's folder (for opencode, a redirect on a group of commands or a pipeline escapes its rules; for Cursor CLI, the edit tool; both wrote outside it when checked). `cornercase bot new --agent opencode` refuses to start without `--access full`.
- **Grok Build** and **Vibe CLI** cannot be a bot yet (`not yet`): Grok was not signed in and Vibe not installed where this was checked.
- **A bot's turn is only what the agent says after its last tool**; the tools it used before show as steps.

<Aside type="caution" title="Full access">
A bot with `full` access can change any file and run any command, with nobody to ask. Give it only to a bot you would trust with an open shell.
</Aside>

## Secrets

A bot that works behind a login needs a password or a token. Saving it in `bot.md` or `memory.md`, or typing it in the chat, leaves it in plain text that every agent of yours can read, so each bot has **secrets**: named values you save for that bot, kept encrypted, which only that bot's own turn reads.

- A secret is a name (1 to 40 characters of `a-z`, `0-9`, `-` and `_`, such as `campus-password`) and a value of 1 to 8,192 bytes. A bot keeps at most 20. A login is two secrets (`campus-user` and `campus-password`), or put the user name in `bot.md`.
- **In the window**, **secrets…** in the bot's menu, or **manage…** in its edit form, opens **Secrets › prs**: the names with when each was saved, **replace…** and **remove** on the selected one (**remove** asks first) and **+ secret**. The value goes into a field that shows dots, is never shown or copied again, and has no copy or cut. The bot's card says `2 secrets`.
- **From the command line**: `printf '%s' "$PASSWORD" | cornercase bot secret set prs campus-password` reads the value from standard input (never from an argument) and replaces a saved one; one line break at the end is dropped. `cornercase bot secret list [BOT] [--json]` prints the names and dates, never a value, `cornercase bot secret remove BOT NAME` (or `--all`) removes, and `cornercase bot secret get NAME` prints a value, **only to the bot itself**: from its own turn, as the brief teaches it. Your own shell, a program and another bot's turn are refused (`only the bot itself reads its secrets`), and so is a bot that tries to save, remove or list another bot's secrets.
- **What cornercase writes**: before a line goes to the chat file, to [`cornercase events`](../scripts-and-agents/), to a toast or a notification, or into a queue entry's result, every value of that bot of 6 bytes or more is replaced by `[secret campus-password]` in its steps, answers and errors (and in the lines about agents it started). This is a convenience, not a guarantee: a value the bot encodes or splits passes, and a value you remove while the bot still holds it is no longer replaced.
- **The file does not open** (another key, a damaged file): the list says `the secrets of prs cannot be read`, nothing is overwritten, and only **remove all** (or `bot secret remove prs --all`) starts again. That removes the bot's secrets, not the key: if `bots.key` itself is damaged or lost, restore it and restart cornercase, and know that deleting it makes a new key and leaves every bot's old secrets unreadable.
- **A secret saved by another client meanwhile is never lost to a stale confirmation**: **remove all** refuses if the list changed since you asked.
- **Never type a secret in the chat.** The chat is a plain file, and a bot told by its brief will not use what you write there: it tells you the value is now in the chat's file and asks for it the right way.
- **The limits**:
  - **The bot reads the value.** It goes to the agent's provider with the conversation, and stays in plain text in the agent's own session file on this computer.
  - **A page or a message the bot reads can tell it to send the value somewhere else.** Give a bot what you would give an assistant, and prefer a token with a narrow scope to a password.
  - **The encryption protects copies**, such as a backup, a synced folder or an agent that searches your disk and reads the file by accident. The key is a file next to the session (`bots.key`, readable only by you), so it does not stop another program of yours that reads both files, a bot with `full` access, or a `cornercase`-access bot that goes looking for the key.
  - On macOS, a command the bot sends to the background whose shell exits is no longer seen as the bot's, and is refused the secret.

### A bot asks for a secret

A bot that needs a password or a token you have not saved for it **asks you for it**, and the value never goes through the chat. Its brief teaches it to run `cornercase bot secret ask campus-user campus-password --about 'to read your grades on the campus site'`, tell you what it asked for and end its answer.

- **The chat** shows `asks for a secret: campus-user, campus-password` with a **give…** button while it waits, the bot's row says `asks for a secret` (`asks` when the sidebar is narrow) and its card has an `asks` row.
- **If you are looking at that bot's chat**, the dialog opens by itself (unless you are typing a message or another dialog is open; then click **give…**). Elsewhere you get a toast, `Campus asks for a secret: campus-user, campus-password` with **go**, and a desktop notification; **go** shows the chat and opens the dialog. While you are [away](../telegram/), Telegram and Discord say `Campus asks for a secret: …; open cornercase to give it`: a value is never taken from a chat app.
- **The dialog**, `Campus asks for a secret`, says the bot's agent and access (`full access` in red), then `It says:` and the bot's own words, as plain text, then one field per name, which shows dots (`replaces the saved one` when it asks to replace a saved secret). **save** keeps every value or none; **not now** (or `Esc`) closes it and the ask waits; **never** turns it down. `Enter` moves to an empty field before it saves. A paste with a tab is refused with the reason.
- **After you save**, the chat says `secret saved: campus-user, campus-password` and the bot is written to at once (`The user saved the secrets you asked for: …`), so it carries on; when the ask came from a [routine](#routines)'s run, nothing starts and the chat is told at your next message. **never** writes `secret not given: …`, and the bot is told at your next message.
- A bot asks for 1 to 4 names at a time, with a reason of at most 200 characters on one line, and keeps at most 4 asks waiting. It cannot ask for a name already saved unless it replaces it (`--replace`); the same names asked again change nothing. Saving one of those names in the Secrets dialog or with `bot secret set` counts. A restart forgets the asks: their lines stay, without **give…**, and the bot asks again. Only the bot itself asks, from its own turn; only you give or decline.

## What is in a bot's folder

Each bot has a folder in `bots/` next to `session.json` (`bots/<id>/`, private to you, `0700`):

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `bot.json` | The bot's settings and the id of its conversation. cornercase rewrites it; it does not read it again while it runs. |
| `bot.md` | What the bot is for. You and the bot both write it: it updates it when you give it a job or change one. `cornercase bot about` prints it. |
| `memory.md` | What it keeps from one conversation to the next. It adds to it, keeps it short and prunes it. |
| `secrets` | The bot's [secrets](#secrets), encrypted, readable only by you; there only while the bot has one. The key is `bots.key` next to `session.json`, not in this folder. |
| `chat.jsonl` | The chat as cornercase shows it, one JSON line per message, step, answer, error, notice or [agent line](#agents-it-starts). It survives `reset` and a change of model. |

The chat is cornercase's own, not the agent's transcript, so what other features write to a bot later lands in the same place. cornercase tells each bot about itself in the first message of a conversation and never writes it to the folder, so a new release updates every bot at its next conversation.

## For programs

Everything is a request over the socket, so a window, a script or a plugin can build on it. `cornercase queue add --bot prs --every 'weekday 09:00' --prompt '…'` schedules a bot's routine, `cornercase queue list --bot prs --json` lists its entries, and inside a bot (`CORNERCASE_BOT` set) a `queue add` with no `--project`, `--workspace` or `--worktree` means that bot. `chat_apps` in `bot new`, `bot edit` and `bot list --json` is the [Telegram and Discord](#on-telegram-and-discord) option (feature `bot-chat-apps`). `cornercase bot about prs --json` gives a program the text of `bot.md`, so its edit form shows what the person is about to change. See [Scripts and agents](../scripts-and-agents/#bots) for the `bot` request, its events and the `bots` of `status --app`.
