cornercase is driven with the mouse. Out of the box it has **no keyboard shortcuts** of its own: outside of its dialogs, the changes panel's filter field, the files panel's search bar and find field and the TODO panel's text field (and `Esc` while you drag a row, a border or a divider, or while the changes, files, TODO or Flow panel is open), every key you press goes to the program in the active pane. That's a deliberate choice, so that nothing you type is ever taken away from your shell, your editor or your agent: `Ctrl+B`, `Ctrl+A`, `Alt` and `Option` combinations all reach them untouched.

If your hands would rather stay on the keyboard, or your terminal can't send clicks, you can turn on a **prefix key** that opens a menu of [keyboard shortcuts](#keyboard-shortcuts). It takes that one key, and only once you choose it.

### Panes

| Gesture | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Click | An inactive pane | Makes it active. The click isn't passed to the program. |
| Right-click | Any pane | Opens the pane menu on release: split right, split down, right-clicks, open in…, close. |
| Right-button drag | From a pane onto another pane | Near an edge, moves the pane to that side of the other one; in the middle, swaps them. See [Move and swap panes](../../guides/panes-and-splits/#move-and-swap-panes). |
| Drag | A divider between panes | Resizes the panes. `Esc` ends the drag where it is. |
| Double-click | A divider | Splits the space in half again. |
| Press, drag, release | Text, when the program doesn't use the mouse | Selects the text and copies it to your clipboard. |
| Wheel | Any pane, when its program doesn't use the mouse | Scrolls back through what it printed, without making it active; in `less`, `man` and other full-screen programs, sends the arrows. See [Scroll back](../../guides/panes-and-splits/#scroll-back). |
| Click | ` ↓ n more ` at the bottom right of a scrolled pane | Goes back to the bottom. So does typing or pasting in it. |
| Hover, then click | A path to a regular file, inside or outside the workspace, in the active pane (including `~/…`) | Underlines it, then opens the file in the [files panel](../../guides/files/#paths-in-your-panes-are-links), at its line when the path has one. |
| Anything | A program that uses the mouse | Passed to the program, relative to its pane. |

Programs that use the mouse get clicks, releases, drags, movement and the wheel, depending on what they asked for, in the encoding they asked for (SGR, UTF-8 or the original X10 one). Right-clicks still open the pane menu, and right-button drags still move panes, unless you chose **send right-clicks to the pane** for that pane. See [Panes and splits](../../guides/panes-and-splits/).

### Dialogs and lists

Click buttons and rows to use them. In lists (the folder picker, the issue browser, the settings, search results) and in an update's release notes, the wheel scrolls. A right-click menu closes when you click anywhere else (a text field's menu closes without its dialog), and so do the search and every dialog, as its cancel button would, so whatever you typed in it is dropped. A dialog that is busy (creating a worktree, installing an update, checking a token, starting an issue) stays open until it is done. A plugin's [popup](../../guides/plugins/#popups) is the exception: a click outside it does nothing, and it closes with its `×`.

### Text fields

| Gesture | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Click | A text field with a cursor | Puts the cursor there and drops any selection. |
| Press, drag, release | A text field | Selects the text; a long field scrolls past its edge while you drag. Typing, `Backspace` and `Delete` replace or remove it. |
| Double-click | A text field | Selects the word under the pointer. |
| Right-click | A text field | Opens a small menu, **copy**, **cut** and **select all**; outside the selection it first moves the cursor there. `Esc` closes it, and a click anywhere else closes only the menu, not the dialog. |

### The resize pointer

Every border that drags (the borders between columns, the line between stacked lists, the line above the agents section, the panel's left border and the dividers between panes) lights up cyan under the mouse. Where the terminal can change the mouse pointer, cornercase also asks for a resize arrow there: ↔ on a border between columns or a divider between panes side by side, ↕ on a line between rows. The arrow stays while you drag, even when the mouse leaves the border, and the usual pointer comes back once the mouse leaves it, when you release the button, when you press `Esc` (which ends the drag where it is), when a dialog opens and when the window quits or detaches.

It works in Ghostty, kitty 0.31 or later and foot 1.12 or later, which cornercase recognises from their answer to its startup question. Other terminals keep their usual pointer: WezTerm, Konsole, Alacritty and GNOME Terminal (VTE) have no way to change it, and iTerm2 and xterm understand other names for the shapes. Inside tmux, screen or Zellij the pointer is never changed: the multiplexer shares the window between panes and would leave the arrow in them.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Off by default. To turn them on, open **Settings → UI**, select **prefix key** and press the combination you want, such as `Ctrl+]`, `Alt+K` or `F12`. It must use `Ctrl` or `Alt` with a key, or be a function key; `Ctrl+C`, `Ctrl+D` and `Ctrl+Z` are refused, and the row warns you when the key you picked already means something, like `Ctrl+B` for tmux or `Ctrl+A` for the start of the line. `Backspace` there turns the shortcuts off again. The key is saved as [`prefix_key`](../configuration/#prefix_key).

Press the prefix and a menu opens at the bottom of the screen with what each next key does. Press one: it runs, and the menu closes. Keys followed by `…` open a group, such as `f` for find, whose menu takes its place, so `f` then `f` finds a file by name. There is nothing to memorise, the menu is always on screen; you can click an entry too.

`Esc`, or a key the menu doesn't list, closes it without doing anything, and that key doesn't reach your program. Press the prefix twice to send the prefix itself to the program in the pane. The prefix works while the pane, the files search or find field, the changes filter or a TODO field has the keys, but not inside a dialog, which has its own keys below.

### A key of your own for a menu entry

Your terminal can send the prefix and the next keys for you, so one combination reaches any entry of the menu. In Ghostty, with `Ctrl+Space` as the prefix (it sends the byte `\x00`), this line in its config opens the settings search:

```
keybind = cmd+shift+p=text:\x00,
```

It replaces Ghostty's own command palette key, so pick another combination if you use that. Other terminals have the same kind of setting for sending text. With another prefix, put the bytes it sends in place of `\x00`.

## Keys inside cornercase's dialogs

While a dialog, the search, the settings, the issue browser, the changes panel's filter field, the files panel's search bar or find field or a TODO field is open, the keys below drive it instead of your program.

| Where | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Any dialog with a text field | Type, `Backspace`, paste. `Enter` confirms, `Esc` cancels. A name (a new workspace or group, a rename) has a cursor: the arrows, `Home` and `End` move it (see below). |
| New workspace | `Tab` toggles **with its own worktree**. |
| A plugin's popup | Every key, `Esc` and the prefix key included, goes to the program in it. Close it with its `×`. |
| Right-click menus | `Esc` closes the menu. Other keys still go to the active pane. A text field's menu closes on any key and `Esc` only closes it; other keys go on to the field. |
| While dragging a row or a pane | `Esc` cancels the drag. Other keys still go to the active pane. |
| While dragging a border or a divider | `Esc` ends the drag where it is. Other keys still go to the active pane. |
| Folder picker | Type to filter. `↑` `↓` select. `→` or `Tab` go into the selected folder, `←` goes up. `Enter` goes into the selected folder, or opens the folder you are in. `~` goes home and `/` to the root when the filter is empty. `Backspace` on an empty filter goes up. |
| Search | Type to search. `Tab` switches between names and what was said in sessions. `↑` `↓` select. `Enter` goes to the result. `Esc` closes. |
| Issue list | Type to filter. `↑` `↓` `PgUp` `PgDn` select. `Enter` reads the issue. `Tab` / `Shift+Tab` switch sources. `Esc` closes. |
| Issue | `Enter` starts it. `↑` `↓` `PgUp` `PgDn` `Space` `Home` `End` scroll. `Esc` or `←` go back. |
| Agent, place and person pickers | Type to filter. `↑` `↓` select. `Enter` picks. `Esc` goes back. |
| Changes panel filter | Type a piece of a path or a pattern: `*.test.js` keeps only those files, `!*.snap` leaves them out. `Enter` keeps the filter and gives the keys back to your program, `Esc` clears it, or closes the panel when the field is empty. A click in a pane gives the keys back too. |
| Files panel search | Type to search. `↑` `↓` pick a result. `Enter` opens it. `Esc` clears the search and gives the keys back to your program, or closes the panel when the bar is empty. A click in a pane gives them back too and keeps the search. |
| TODO field | Type, paste. `←` `→` move the cursor, `Ctrl+←` `Ctrl+→` (or `Option+←` `Option+→`) a word at a time, `↑` `↓` move across the lines of a long item, `Home` `End` (or `Ctrl+A` `Ctrl+E`) go to its start and end, `Backspace` `Delete` delete. `Enter` saves (a new item keeps the field open for the next one), `Esc` cancels, or closes the panel when the field is empty. In every field with a cursor (a TODO item, the schedule form, a prompt or a flow's text, a project's setup and teardown, the paste field in Settings › Share, the tour's prompt, the name of a new workspace or group and a rename), `Ctrl+←` and `Ctrl+→` move by word and stop at line breaks. `Option+←` and `Option+→` do the same, in these fields only: a terminal sends them as `Alt+←` `Alt+→` or as `Esc b` and `Esc f`, and both work (on a Mac, a terminal may need Option set to send Esc or Meta). On macOS `Ctrl+←` and `Ctrl+→` switch desktops unless you turn off **Move left a space** and **Move right a space** in System Settings › Keyboard › Keyboard Shortcuts › Mission Control; Option needs no change.

`Home` and `End` go to the start and end of the line, and so do `Ctrl+A` and `Ctrl+E`, in these fields only. That is what `Cmd+←` and `Cmd+→` send in Ghostty on a Mac, so they work there too; outside these fields `Ctrl+A` and `Ctrl+E` still reach the program in the pane.

To select with the keys, hold `Shift` with `←` `→` `↑` `↓`, `Home` or `End`; with `Ctrl+Shift` or `Option+Shift` it goes a word at a time. A plain arrow drops the selection (`←` to its start, `→` to its end), and `Home` or `End` drops it too. Copy and cut come from the right-click menu, and your terminal's paste replaces the selection. `Shift`+click cannot extend a selection, because terminals keep it for their own. Some terminals take `Ctrl+Shift+←` `Ctrl+Shift+→` for their tabs (Ghostty on Linux with several tabs, kitty); `Option+Shift` is the alternative there.

## Keys in your panes

Everything else goes to the program in the active pane, encoded like a modern terminal would:

- When a program enables the **kitty keyboard protocol**, cornercase encodes every key with it, using Ghostty's encoder.
- Otherwise special keys go through Ghostty's encoder and ordinary characters through the classic encoding, so old programs keep working.
- cornercase asks your own terminal to report keys unambiguously, so combinations such as `Shift+Enter` and `Ctrl+Enter` keep their modifiers, as long as your terminal supports it.
- Pastes are wrapped in bracketed-paste markers when the program asked for them.

<Aside type="note">
Programs inside cornercase never receive key releases, even with the kitty protocol. That matters only for the rare program that reacts to them, such as some games.
</Aside>

In Processes, `↑` `↓` pick a line, `→` and `←` open and fold a pane, `Enter` shows the pane (or asks to stop the process on a process line), `Tab` changes the sort, and any other key types in the filter; `Esc` clears the filter, then closes it.

In Usage, **Limits** and **Stats** switch by click, `Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `←` or `→`. It opens on Stats and remembers the last tab for the server run. `Enter`, `Esc`, `done` or a click outside closes it.
